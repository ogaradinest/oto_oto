It's not a new app, it's already where you're working

Copilot sits inside Word, Excel, Outlook and Teams rather than living in its own tab

Most AI tools make you open a separate website, copy your work in, wait, then copy it back out. Copilot skips that: it's built into Microsoft 365. Ask it to draft a client email in Outlook, summarise a thread in Teams, or build a formula in Excel, and it works inside the document itself, one less tool to log into.

Take a one-person consultancy pulling together a client proposal in Word. Instead of writing the first draft from a blank page, they describe the brief to Copilot, get a structured first pass in under a minute, then spend their time tightening the argument, turning a 90-minute task into closer to 30.

What Microsoft's own numbers actually show

The headline stats come from Microsoft's research into its earliest users, not a marketing claim

Microsoft's Work Trend Index tested Copilot on the same writing, searching and summarising tasks, with and without it switched on. Users were 29% faster across the board, and nearly four times quicker on one search-heavy task. Self-reported numbers agree: 73% said they completed tasks faster, 70% felt more productive overall. Not every task gets easier, but for the repetitive admin that eats a founder's week, it's worth testing on your own work.

What it costs, and where it fits

The Copilot add-on starts from $21 a user a month on an annual plan, on top of your existing Microsoft 365 subscription

Copilot isn't free, it's an add-on to Microsoft 365 Business plans, from $21 per user, per month, paid yearly ($25.20 monthly), or bundled into Business Premium. For a founder already paying for Microsoft 365, that's a smaller jump than a brand-new subscription. Watching every pound? Try the limited free Copilot Chat built into most Business plans first.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Do I need to already use Microsoft 365 for Copilot to work?

Yes, the paid add-on sits on top of an existing Microsoft 365 Business subscription. Not using Word, Excel or Outlook already? A standalone AI assistant will suit you better.

Does Microsoft use my business data to train its AI?

Microsoft says Copilot doesn't use your organisation's prompts or data to train its models for other customers. Still, check your plan's data terms before putting anything sensitive through it.

How is this different from just using ChatGPT?

ChatGPT is a separate app you copy text into and out of. Copilot works inside the document you're already editing, saving the copy-paste step and keeping formatting intact.

Is it worth it for a one-person startup?

Trial the free Copilot Chat first. Add the paid tier once you can point to specific weekly tasks, proposals, reports, inbox triage, where the time saved covers the cost.

Why this matters for you

For a small business, the real cost of admin isn't the software bill, it's the founder's own time, the scarcest resource in the building. A tool that lives inside apps you already pay for has a lower bar to actually get used day to day than something brand new. That matters more for a two-person company than a 200-person one, because there's no one else to hand the admin to.
