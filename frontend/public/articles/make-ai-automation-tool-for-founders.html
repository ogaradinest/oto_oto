What Make actually does

A visual map of if-this-then-that, with AI steps built in

Open Make and you get a canvas, not a chat box. You drag in "triggers", a new Stripe payment, a new form reply, a new email, and connect them to "actions", add a row, send a message, draft a reply, by wiring them together like a very patient robot assistant. Recent versions bundle AI modules too, so a step can ask a model to summarise a message or sort an enquiry before passing it on, without opening a separate tool.

Picture a one-person candle business run out of a spare room. Before Make, she spent roughly 40 minutes most mornings copying yesterday's Etsy orders into a spreadsheet and sending a handwritten thank-you email to each buyer. One scenario, built from a free Make template in an afternoon, now does both the moment an order lands, and flags anything over £50 for a personal note instead. That's the whole pitch: not more software, just the plumbing between the software you already have.

What it costs, and the bit nobody mentions

Pricing runs on "credits", not on how many apps you connect

Make's free plan gives you 1,000 credits a month, with each action in a scenario, not each app, spending one credit, and over 3,000 apps are available even on that free tier. The cheapest paid plan, Core, starts at $12 a month for 10,000 credits once you outgrow that, which for most two or three-step scenarios is still a small line item.

The bit nobody mentions: building a scenario is easy, but debugging one that half-works is where founders actually lose an afternoon. Test with two or three real records before switching an automation on for everything, and expect your first attempt at anything with more than three steps to need a second pass.

Quick Answers

The questions founders ask most

Do I need to know how to code to use Make?

No. It's drag-and-drop, though filters and conditional logic (only do this if that's true) take a few tries to get right the first time.

How much does Make cost?

Free for up to 1,000 credits a month. Paid plans start at $12 a month for 10,000 credits, billed on how many actions you run, not how many apps you connect.

Is Make better than Zapier?

Neither wins outright. Zapier is quicker to start with its templates, while Make tends to work out cheaper once you're running more than a handful of automations and don't mind a slightly steeper learning curve.

What happens if an automation breaks halfway through?

Make flags the failed run and lets you fix and replay that single run, so one bad entry doesn't force you to redo everything that ran before it.

Why this matters for you

A founder starting out rarely has budget for an ops hire, so every manual handoff between apps comes out of the founder's own unpaid evening hours. The Visa figure above, that 90% of small businesses are already weighing automation tools, suggests this has stopped being a nice-to-have.
