BCG's AI at Work 2025 research found that the share of employees who feel positive about generative AI rises from 15% to 55% when leadership visibly supports it, a dramatic jump. The catch is that only around one-quarter of frontline employees say they actually receive that visible support. Russell Reynolds Associates' Global Leadership Monitor found leaders' own confidence moving the right way, up from 35% to 44% over a year when asked whether their team could align resources around GenAI, with the share of leaders piloting or fully implementing GenAI in their own function rising to 49%, up from 29% the year before. Confidence is improving among the people at the top. It is not reaching most of the people beneath them yet.

Expleo's UK business confidence index slipped from a peak of 74 in September 2025 to 67 in April 2026, even as adoption kept climbing. Confidence and adoption are not the same curve, and a small business owner should not assume rising usage means rising trust in it.

Confidence is a leadership problem before it is a technology problem

What the BCG and Russell Reynolds numbers show together

The two data sets fit together in an uncomfortable way. Leaders are getting somewhat more confident in their own ability to steer AI adoption, and more of them are piloting or fully implementing it inside their own function. But BCG's finding that only around a quarter of frontline employees report receiving visible leadership support means that growing confidence at the top is mostly staying at the top. Staff are left to work out whether the business actually believes in the tool it just handed them.

Expleo's confidence index adds a caution to the picture: even where usage keeps rising, the UK-wide confidence measure has been falling since its September 2025 peak. A team using AI more often is not automatically a team that trusts it more, and a leader who assumes otherwise is likely to misread quiet compliance as genuine buy-in.

What visible support actually looks like for one person

No change management team required

At small business scale, visible leadership support is not a programme, it is a handful of repeated habits. Using the tool yourself, in front of staff, rather than only mandating it for others. Talking about a failed attempt openly instead of only sharing the wins, since staff read silence about failure as a sign the whole thing is not meant to be questioned. Naming one shared metric everyone can see, so the tool's success or failure is a shared fact rather than a private judgement made somewhere else.

The common failure pattern is the opposite of all three: an owner announces a new AI tool once, in an email or a quick meeting, and is never seen using it again. Staff are left to work out the details themselves, and BCG's frontline support figure suggests most businesses fall into exactly this pattern without meaning to.

A five-minute monthly habit that counts as real support

Small, repeated, and visible beats one big announcement

Set a recurring five minutes, once a month, where you tell the team one specific thing AI helped with and one specific thing it got wrong or wasted time on. This single habit, repeated, is what turns a leader's private confidence into something a team can actually see and respond to. It costs nothing beyond the time, and it directly targets the exact gap BCG measured between leadership confidence and frontline experience of that confidence.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Does leadership support for AI really change how employees feel about it?

Substantially, according to BCG's research. Employee positivity about GenAI rises from 15% to 55% where leadership support is visible, though only around a quarter of frontline employees currently report receiving that support.

Is leadership confidence in AI actually rising?

Yes, gradually. Russell Reynolds Associates found leaders' confidence in their team's ability to align resources around GenAI rose from 35% to 44% over a year, with the share of leaders piloting or fully implementing GenAI in their own function reaching 49%.

Why does confidence in AI sometimes fall even as adoption rises?

Expleo's UK confidence index fell from a peak of 74 in September 2025 to 67 in April 2026 despite continued adoption growth, showing usage and trust move on different timelines. More people using a tool does not automatically mean more people trust it.

What does visible AI leadership support look like in a small business?

Using the tool yourself where staff can see it, talking openly about a failed attempt rather than only the wins, and naming one shared metric everyone can track. None of this requires a formal change management process.

Why this matters for you

A small business owner is both the leader BCG's research describes and, functionally, part of the frontline that rarely sees genuine support. That double role is actually an advantage: closing the gap between leadership confidence and frontline experience does not require cascading a message through several management layers, only choosing to be visibly consistent about using and discussing the tool with the two or three people who work alongside you.

Try this next

This week, tell your team one specific thing AI has helped with recently and one specific thing it got wrong, in person or in a short message. Repeat that same five-minute update once a month. It is the single cheapest way to close the gap between how confident you feel about AI and how much support your team actually sees.
