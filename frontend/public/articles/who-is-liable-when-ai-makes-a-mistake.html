The Myth: It's the Software's Problem, Not Yours

Spoiler: it almost never works that way

Plenty of owners assume that if an AI tool quotes the wrong price, drafts a misleading reply, or mishandles a customer's details, the blame sits with whoever built the tool. It's an easy assumption to make, since you didn't write a line of its code.

In practice, your customers deal with you, not your software vendor. If an AI chatbot tells someone they're entitled to a refund that doesn't exist, that's generally treated as your business making the claim, the same as if a staff member had said it on the phone.

Picture a small bakery that lets its AI chatbot answer website questions. One evening it tells a customer their order is "fully refundable, no questions asked", a policy the bakery has never actually offered. The owner now has a real complaint on their hands, and a vendor contract that almost certainly puts the job of checking outputs back on the business, not the software.

What You're Actually on the Hook For

Two areas worth knowing before you worry about the rest

If an AI tool touches customer data, UK GDPR still applies to you, not just to the software. A genuine personal data breach needs reporting to the ICO, usually within 72 hours, and that clock doesn't care whose chatbot caused it.

If AI plays any part in hiring or eligibility decisions, the Equality Act 2010 still applies too. "The AI decided" isn't a defence if the outcome turns out discriminatory, and a rushed rubber-stamp from a human doesn't count as genuine oversight either.

Quick Answers

The questions owners ask most

Is my AI vendor liable if their tool gives a customer bad advice?

Rarely as the first port of call. Most vendor terms place responsibility for reviewing outputs on you, the business using the tool, so check that clause before you lean on it.

Does having a human check AI outputs protect me completely?

Only if the check is real. A rushed glance before hitting send doesn't count as genuine human oversight under the rules covering automated decisions.

What counts as a data breach if my AI tool mishandles customer details?

If personal data is exposed or misused, it's treated the same as any other breach under UK GDPR, with a duty to report notifiable ones to the ICO, usually within 72 hours.

Do I need a solicitor before using any AI tool?

Not for low-risk internal tasks like drafting notes. For anything customer-facing or touching personal data, an hour reviewing your terms and privacy notice is worth it.

Why this matters for you

Big companies have legal teams to absorb this kind of risk quietly in the background. You don't, which makes "it's not my problem" a far more expensive assumption for a small business than for a corporation with compliance staff on payroll. A customer complaint or a data slip lands directly on your desk, and your time, not a department's.
