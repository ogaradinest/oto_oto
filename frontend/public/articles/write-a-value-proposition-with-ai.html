Why 'We Help Businesses With AI' Says Nothing

A value proposition names one outcome, for one person, in one sentence

A value proposition isn't a tagline or a mission statement. It's a single sentence that answers three things at once: who this is for, what specific problem you fix for them, and why that beats whatever they're doing now. If a stranger can't repeat your business back to you after reading it once, it isn't working yet, however clever it sounds in the boardroom.

Take a one-person bookkeeping startup. The founder's homepage read 'cloud bookkeeping for growing businesses' for its first month live, and brought in zero sign-ups. After running real customer conversations through an AI prompt, she rewrote it to 'Catch up three years of messy VAT bookkeeping in two weeks, fixed price, no judgement.' Same traffic, same price, four paying clients the following month, because it finally named a problem instead of a category.

Build It in an Hour With AI, Not a Branding Sprint

Feed it your customers' real words, not your own guesses

Pull five to ten real customer quotes, complaints or messages from people who've asked about your idea. Paste them into ChatGPT or Claude along with a value proposition canvas prompt: list the customer's pains and desired gains, then your product's pain relievers and gain creators, then draft five one-sentence value propositions in different framings, outcome-led, problem-led, and head-to-head against the obvious alternative.

Don't just pick the line you personally like best. Put two or three candidates in a homepage headline or an email subject line, or just say them out loud to five real prospects, and watch for 'tell me more' rather than a polite nod. The version that gets people leaning in, not the one that sounds most impressive to you, is the one to keep.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

What's the actual difference between a value proposition and a slogan?

A slogan is memorable; a value proposition is testable. If you can't rephrase it as 'because of [you], [this customer] gets [this outcome] instead of [the alternative]', it's a slogan, not a value proposition.

Can AI really write this instead of hiring a branding consultant?

For a strong first draft, yes. AI is good at turning scattered customer language into clean sentence options fast, but it still needs real customers' words as input, not just your own assumptions about them.

How many value propositions does one business need?

Usually one for the whole company, though you may need a slightly different phrasing per landing page or audience if you genuinely sell to more than one kind of customer.

What if I don't have customers yet to quote?

Use the language from building your ideal customer profile instead, draft from that, then validate the result live with five target customers before you build anything else around it.

Why this matters for you

A small business rarely has a sales team to explain things in person, so the written line on your homepage, pitch and first cold email often is the whole business, as far as a stranger is concerned. With most B2B buyers now forming an opinion before any conversation happens, a vague value proposition isn't cosmetic, it's lost revenue you never see because people simply never got in touch.
