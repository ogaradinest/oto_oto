So you're finally hiring your first person. Exciting, terrifying, and you've got a blank job ad staring back at you with no idea what to type. Here's what nobody tells first-time founders: the wording on that ad decides who even bothers applying, before a single CV lands in your inbox. Get it wrong and you quietly filter out your best candidates without meaning to. AI fixes the blank-page problem in minutes, but only if you use it right.

Your job ad is quietly filtering people out

Most founders don't even realise they're doing it

Take Priya, who runs a six-person landscaping business outside Reading. Her first ad for an office coordinator asked for someone "driven, competitive, and able to dominate a chaotic workload." Three men applied. Zero women. She hadn't set out to write a male-coded ad; that's just how urgency comes out when you're typing fast at 11pm.

Run the same ad through an AI tool and ask it specifically to flag gendered or exclusionary language, and it catches words like "dominate", "ninja" and "rockstar" in seconds, words that read as confident to one group and off-putting to another.

How to actually do this with AI

Four prompts, fifteen minutes, done

Open any AI chat tool you already have, ChatGPT or Claude both work fine, and describe the role the way you'd describe it to a mate: what the person will actually do day to day, not a wish list of buzzwords. Ask for a first draft.

Then ask it for three fixes: strip out jargon and inflated requirements ("5 years' experience" for entry-level admin is a classic), flag gendered or exclusionary language, and shorten it to the essentials a candidate needs to decide whether to apply. Read it out loud. If it doesn't sound human, tell the AI to make it sound like you.

What to keep human

AI drafts it, you decide it

Don't let AI invent salary bands, legal requirements, or a long list of "nice to haves" that scares off good people. Check pay against something like the UK National Living Wage and your own numbers, and keep the actual requirements to what the job needs on day one, not day 400.

Quick Answers

The questions first-time founders ask most

Will an AI-written job ad sound robotic?

Only if you let it. Ask the AI to match your tone, then rewrite the first line yourself so it sounds like you, not a template.

Do I need a specific AI hiring tool, or will ChatGPT do?

A general AI chat tool is plenty for writing the ad itself. Dedicated hiring tools help later, with screening CVs, but you don't need one just to draft a job post.

How long should a job description actually be?

Short enough that a candidate can read it on their phone in under two minutes. If it's longer than that, you're probably listing wishes, not requirements.

Is it legal to use AI-generated job description wording?

Yes, but you're still responsible for what it says. Check pay, hours, and any legal requirements, like right-to-work wording, yourself before you post it.

Why this matters for you

For a small business, a badly worded ad doesn't just mean fewer applicants, it means fewer of the right ones, which means a slower hire and a bigger gap in the business while the role sits empty. At an average £35,500 first-year cost for a first UK hire, getting it right the first time isn't a nice-to-have.
