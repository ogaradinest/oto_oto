The skills gap: training exists, but not where it is needed

A paradox between stated effort and actual readiness

97% of organisations report an AI skills gap, and over 90% of global enterprises are projected to face critical skills shortages by 2026, a gap risking an estimated $5.5 trillion in lost economic value globally. Yet 82% of enterprise leaders say their organisation provides some form of AI training. The disconnect is in how that training is delivered: only 35% of leaders report a mature, organisation-wide upskilling programme, with most training fragmented, optional and disconnected from the actual tasks staff need to do.

For UK small businesses specifically, two in three employees have had no formal AI training at all, and only 31% of UK SMEs have adopted AI meaningfully. The UK government's AI Skills for Business programme, launched January 2026, now offers free benchmarked AI courses to all UK adults, and a separate £6.4 million Flexible AI Upskilling Fund covers up to 50% of training costs for eligible businesses, a genuine, funded route to closing this specific gap.

The governance gap: policies exist, accountability often does not

Fewer businesses with no policy, but gaps remain in what those policies cover

The share of businesses with no responsible AI policy in place fell sharply from 24% to 11%, a real improvement, and dedicated AI governance roles grew 17% in 2025. But having a policy document is not the same as having working accountability: many businesses still lack a specific process for monitoring how an AI tool behaves in practice, reviewing decisions it makes, or assigning responsibility when it produces a harmful or wrong outcome.

For a small business, this gap is usually not about writing the wrong policy, it is about not writing one specific enough to answer a real question when something goes wrong: who is told, who decides what happens next, and how the mistake gets corrected. A policy that only states general principles without these specifics provides little help in the moment it is actually needed.

The infrastructure gap: dark data undermines everything built on top of it

AI is only as good as the data it can actually see

Lack of talent is cited as a barrier by 46% of organisations, data privacy concerns by 43%, and poor data quality by 40%, a cluster of related problems that often trace back to the same root cause for small businesses: dark data, information that exists somewhere in the organisation but is hidden, unstructured, or simply not connected to anything an AI tool can use.

Fragmented, unclassified data does not just limit what an AI tool can do, it actively creates the specific failure modes small businesses fear most: confident but wrong answers and security exposure from data nobody realised was accessible. Fixing this rarely means buying new infrastructure outright. It usually starts with the more basic work of cataloguing what data actually exists and where.

A realistic order for closing these three gaps

Skills first, since funding now covers part of the cost

For a small business, skills is the most immediately actionable gap given the current funding available: the free AI Skills for Business courses and the £6.4 million upskilling fund covering up to half of training costs make this the lowest-cost gap to start closing right now, with AI apprenticeships fully funded for under-25s from August 2026.

Governance and infrastructure both take longer to fix properly, but neither requires an enterprise-scale solution for a small business. A specific, written escalation process for AI mistakes and a basic inventory of where your business's data actually lives are both achievable without a dedicated team, and both directly address the two gaps least likely to be closed by any external funding programme.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

How widespread is the AI skills gap really?

97% of organisations report some form of AI skills gap, and two in three UK employees have had no formal AI training, despite 82% of leaders saying their organisation provides some training.

Is UK government funding available to help close the skills gap?

Yes. The AI Skills for Business programme, launched January 2026, offers free benchmarked courses, and a £6.4 million Flexible AI Upskilling Fund covers up to 50% of training costs for eligible SMEs, alongside fully funded AI apprenticeships for under-25s from August 2026.

What is the governance gap, specifically?

Many businesses have a written AI policy but lack a specific process for monitoring how a tool behaves, reviewing its decisions, or assigning responsibility when something goes wrong, even though the share with no policy at all has fallen from 24% to 11%.

What is dark data and why does it matter for AI readiness?

Data that exists somewhere in an organisation but is hidden, unstructured or disconnected from any system an AI tool can use. It is a major driver of the poor data quality and privacy concerns cited by 40% and 43% of organisations respectively as barriers to AI adoption.

Why this matters for you

A small business faces the same three readiness gaps as a large enterprise, skills, governance and data infrastructure, but with genuinely funded routes to closing at least the first one: free courses and grants covering up to half of training costs. Treating these three gaps as separate, specific problems, rather than one vague AI readiness issue, gives a small business a concrete starting point for each, rather than an overwhelming, undifferentiated task.
