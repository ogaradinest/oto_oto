Adoption Climbs, Proof Lags

Two credible surveys, two adoption numbers, one shared problem

KPMG's Global AI Pulse survey found that 26% of UK companies now use AI as part of everyday work, up from 18% at the start of 2026, a genuine jump in six months. The UK government's own numbers, from DSIT's national AI Adoption Research (IFF Research and Technopolis Group, surveying 3,500 UK businesses), put overall business AI use at 21%, using a broader definition of "use."

Both surveys agree on the harder number: proving it was worth it. KPMG found only 7% of leaders report established ROI from AI, even though 24% say they're under direct pressure from investors to demonstrate value. Adoption has moved faster than measurement.

The Cost Visibility Problem

It is hard to prove ROI on spend you can't see

KPMG's data points to a specific, fixable cause. Most UK leaders don't have a clear picture of what they're actually spending on AI. 42% report only partial visibility into their AI costs, and 30% say usage-based pricing models make that worse: the more AI gets embedded into workflows, the harder it becomes to attribute spend to outcomes.

42% have only partial cost visibility 30% struggle with usage-based pricing 4x more likely to show ROI with strong visibility

The payoff for fixing this is significant. Organisations with strong cost visibility are four times more likely to report established ROI than those without it, at 25% versus 6%. That single gap explains more of the UK's ROI shortfall than any capability or model-quality issue.

Underinvest, Under-Deliver

What PwC's 2026 AI Performance Study found when it compared the UK to the world

PwC's 2026 AI Performance Study found that globally, nearly three-quarters (74%) of AI's economic value is being captured by just one-fifth (20%) of organisations, a small group of leaders pulling steadily ahead of everyone still in pilot mode. Quick Answers

The questions UK business leaders ask most

What percentage of UK businesses use AI in 2026?

Around a quarter. KPMG's mid-2026 Global AI Pulse survey put day-to-day AI use among UK companies at 26%, up from 18% at the start of the year. DSIT's broader national survey puts overall business AI use at 21%.

Why is AI ROI so hard to prove?

Mostly cost visibility. KPMG found only 7% of UK leaders have established AI ROI, and that organisations with strong visibility into AI spend are four times more likely to report it, at 25% versus 6%. 42% of leaders say they only have partial visibility into what they're spending.

Why does the UK lag global AI leaders on returns?

Investment level. PwC's 2026 AI Performance Study found UK leading companies invest around 2% of revenue in AI against roughly 5% for the world's top performers, and see about 10% returns versus 15% globally.

What's actually stopping UK businesses from adopting AI?

Mostly skills and clarity, ahead of cost or regulation. DSIT's survey found 97% of organisations report an AI skills gap. Among non-adopters, "no identified need" (71%) and "limited AI skills" (60%) are cited far more often than cost or regulation.

Why this matters for you

These are national numbers, but the pattern inside them is exactly what shows up in a small business: someone signs up for an AI tool, uses it for a few weeks, and nobody ever checks whether it actually saved time or made money. You don't need a finance team or a data platform to close that gap. You need one number, tracked before and after, for one workflow at a time.

Try this next

Pick the single AI tool your business already pays for or uses most. Write down one thing it should be saving you, whether that's hours per week, faster replies to customers, or fewer errors in a recurring task. Track that one number for two weeks. If you can't see an improvement, either change how the tool is used or stop paying for it. This is the entire cost-visibility fix from the KPMG data, scaled down to one tool and one owner.
