Before Go-Live

The checklist we run before any system goes live for a client

  • Data access tested with the same permissions the live system will have, not your admin account
  • A fallback path for when the AI is wrong, confused, or down
  • One person, by name, told plainly that if this breaks, it's their problem first
  • A defined success metric, agreed before go-live, not invented afterward to justify the spend

Why Most Implementations Stall

Gartner's breakdown of what leaders blame after a failure

Gartner surveyed infrastructure and operations leaders and asked what actually went wrong when an AI initiative failed. The answers cluster around three things, and none of them are about model quality.

57%

Unrealistic expectations

Leaders who blamed a failure said they expected too much, too fast.

38%

Poor data quality

Limited or unreliable data was a direct cause of the project failing.

38%

Skill gaps

Persistent gaps in the team's skills kept getting in the way.

Gartner predicts that through 2026, 60% of AI projects will be abandoned specifically because the underlying data was never made ready for AI use in the first place. That's a data problem discovered during implementation, not a model problem.

What the 28% Do Differently

Integration and trust, not a better model

Among the leaders who did deliver at least one successful AI use case, 77% credited two things above all else: integrating the tool into an existing workflow rather than bolting it on the side, and having full support from the business executives who own that workflow.

77% credit workflow integration and exec support 57% of high-maturity orgs report business units trust the AI Only 14% report the same trust at low-maturity orgs

That trust gap compounds. In organisations with high AI maturity, 57% say business units trust and are ready to use new AI tools. In low-maturity organisations, only 14% say the same. Trust doesn't happen after go-live. It's built during it, through exactly the checklist above.

Governance Isn't a Phase Two

By the time "later" arrives, it's already too late

Teams often treat governance as something to add later, once the system is proven. By the time later arrives, the system is already making decisions nobody can fully explain, and retrofitting a log or an owner onto a live system is far harder than building it in from day one. Quick Answers

What comes up before every go-live

What's the single biggest reason AI implementations fail?

Unrealistic expectations, cited by 57% of leaders who reported a failure, according to Gartner. Poor data quality and skill gaps follow closely, each cited by 38%.

How much does data quality actually matter?

Enough that Gartner predicts 60% of AI projects will be abandoned through 2026 specifically because the data behind them was never made AI-ready. Test data access with real, restricted permissions before go-live, not your admin account.

Do we need executive support for a small AI project?

Yes. It's one of the two factors that 77% of successful projects credit, alongside integrating the tool into an existing workflow rather than running it as a side project.

When should governance start?

Before go-live, not after. A named owner, a readable log, and a fallback path all need to exist on day one, not once something has already gone wrong.

Why this matters for you

A small business can't absorb a failed implementation the way a large enterprise can shrug one off and try again next quarter. You likely have one shot at this workflow, one budget, and one team's patience. The checklist above exists to make sure that shot counts.
