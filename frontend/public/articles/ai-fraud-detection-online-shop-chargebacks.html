Small shops are the easy target, not the big ones

You don't have a fraud team. Fraudsters know exactly that.

A big retailer has a fraud desk. You have you, on your phone, between everything else. That gap is exactly what fraudsters test for — stolen cards get run through small checkouts first.

Mollie found UK SMBs spend 15 working days a year just managing fraud: disputing chargebacks, refunding "friendly fraud" claims, chasing carding attempts. Three working weeks you didn't choose to spend.

Picture a small homeware shop turning over £15,000 a month online. Before screening: 3-4 chargebacks a month, costing roughly £600 in lost stock and fees, plus hours each week disputing cases that mostly don't get won back.

What an AI fraud tool actually does at checkout

It's not a bouncer turning people away — it's a quiet background check.

Tools like Stripe Radar or Signifyd score every order the second it's placed: does the billing address match the card, has this device hit five other stores today, is the order oddly large for a first-time buyer. Suspicious orders get held for a quick manual look instead of shipping automatically.

After the homeware shop turned on AI screening inside their existing Stripe checkout, no new software, no developer, fraudulent orders dropped to about one a month. Roughly £450 a month back, plus the hours that used to go into disputes.

The point isn't to block more people. It's to stop guessing, instead of either accepting everything and eating the losses, or reviewing everything by hand and slowing down real customers.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Will this block real customers by mistake?

A few genuine orders get flagged at first, that's normal. Most tools let you set the risk threshold, so you start cautious and loosen it once you see your real fraud pattern.

Do I need a developer to set this up?

No. On Stripe, Shopify, or Square, fraud screening is usually a toggle already built into the checkout you're using.

What does it cost?

Many basic tools are free, bundled into your payment processor. Dedicated services like Signifyd charge a small percentage per transaction, usually worth it once fraud is already costing you more.

What's the difference between fraud and a chargeback?

Fraud is someone using a stolen card. A chargeback is the dispute that follows, sometimes real fraud, sometimes a customer who forgot they bought the thing. Screening mostly stops the first, which cuts the second.

Why this matters for you

A big company can absorb a bad month of chargebacks. A small shop can't — £10,800 is real margin for most businesses this size, and the 15 days a year spent fighting it is time nobody was budgeting for. The tools that used to belong to enterprise fraud teams are now a setting inside the checkout software most small businesses already use, which means the barrier isn't cost anymore. It's just not knowing the switch exists.
