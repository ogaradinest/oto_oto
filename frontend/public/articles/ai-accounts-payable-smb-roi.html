Almost every accounts payable automation case study quotes savings from a business processing tens of thousands of invoices a year. That is not most small businesses. If your finance team processes a few hundred invoices a month, the maths still works, just differently. Manual invoice processing typically costs businesses somewhere between £8 and £30 per invoice once you count data entry, approval chasing and exception handling. Automated processing brings that down to roughly £1 to £3. Below a certain volume, though, the software cost can outweigh the saving.

The honest threshold sits around 250 invoices a month. Above that, automation usually pays for itself within a year. Below it, start with the free invoice-capture features already built into your accounting software before paying for a dedicated platform.

What automation actually changes in the invoice workflow

From manual entry to touchless processing

A traditional invoice arrives by email, post or supplier portal. Someone keys the data into the accounting system, checks it against a purchase order, routes it for approval and schedules payment. Each handoff adds time and a chance for error. AI-based capture reads the invoice regardless of format, extracts the vendor, amount, due date and line items, and checks it automatically against your purchase order and delivery record.

The point of this is not eliminating the finance team. It is removing the data entry and matching steps so a person only looks at exceptions: mismatched amounts, unusual vendors, or anything outside your normal approval pattern.

Why invoice fraud makes this more than an efficiency project

The numbers behind the risk

UK Finance's 2026 fraud report puts total payment fraud losses at £1.28 billion for 2025, with invoice and mandate fraud specifically accounting for £41.3 million across just over 2,300 reported cases. Fake invoices, a slightly misspelled vendor name, or a legitimate supplier's bank details changed by a fraudster are the classic patterns, and they are hard for a busy finance person checking invoices manually to catch consistently.

Automated matching does not remove judgement from the process. It makes the checking consistent. A system trained on your normal supplier patterns flags a new bank account for an established vendor or an invoice well above the usual amount before payment goes out, rather than after.

Two regulatory deadlines that make 2026 the year to sort this out

Making Tax Digital and e-invoicing

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax becomes mandatory from 6 April 2026 for sole traders and landlords with qualifying income above £50,000, with the first quarterly update due by 7 August 2026. Digital record-keeping stops being optional at that point, and an AP system that captures and stores invoices digitally by default does most of that work for you already.

Separately, the UK confirmed at Budget 2025 that e-invoicing will become mandatory for business-to-business and public sector VAT invoices from 1 April 2029, using the Peppol network. That deadline sits further out, but AP platforms with e-invoicing support built in avoid a second migration project a few years from now.

Whether the numbers actually work for your invoice volume

Do the maths before you buy

Dedicated AP automation platforms typically cost from a few thousand pounds a year for smaller teams to tens of thousands for larger ones, with implementation adding 10 to 20% on top. Businesses processing under roughly 250 invoices a month often find that cost outweighs the saving from a specialist platform, at least initially.

The practical order is: check what your existing accounting software already captures automatically, measure your actual monthly invoice volume and current cost per invoice for a month, and only then compare that number against a specialist platform's quote. Most small finance teams skip the middle step and either overpay for a platform they do not need yet, or miss a saving that was already available for free.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

How many invoices a month justifies a dedicated AP automation platform?

Roughly 250 invoices a month is the threshold industry data points to. Below that, the platform cost can outweigh the time saved, and the free capture features in your existing accounting software are usually enough.

Does AP automation stop invoice fraud completely?

No. It makes checking consistent rather than occasional, flagging a changed bank account or an unusual amount before payment, which is where most invoice fraud succeeds against a manual process.

Do we need to worry about e-invoicing yet?

Not urgently. The mandate applies from 1 April 2029 for B2B and public sector VAT invoices. It is worth checking that any AP platform you choose now has e-invoicing support on its roadmap, so you are not migrating twice.

Does Making Tax Digital affect how we handle accounts payable?

Indirectly, yes. MTD for Income Tax requires digital record-keeping from 6 April 2026 for qualifying sole traders and landlords. An AP system that captures invoices digitally by default supports that requirement without extra work.

Why this matters for you
