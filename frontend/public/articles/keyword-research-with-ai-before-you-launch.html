Why chasing "coffee shop" wastes your first year

Broad keywords are a losing game for a brand-new site

Take a founder launching a mobile dog-grooming van. "Dog groomer" gets searched a lot, and a dozen national platforms plus every established local groomer already sit on page one. "Mobile dog groomer that comes to your house in Wakefield" gets searched a fraction as often, but almost nobody targets it directly, and whoever types it is closer to booking than anyone typing "dog groomer" ever is.

That's the whole trade-off in one example. Long-tail phrases have less search volume each, but there are so many of them, and so little competition for most, that a brand-new site can realistically rank for a handful within weeks rather than years. Broad, one- or two-word terms are the ones every competitor already owns.

How to run it with AI in about an hour

Two free tools, one afternoon, no subscription needed

Start by describing your business to ChatGPT or Claude in a few sentences, what you sell, who buys it, what problem it solves, and ask for 30 long-tail phrases your ideal customer might actually type, framed as questions and specific problems rather than single words. You'll get a mix of strong and weak ideas, which is fine, this is a brainstorm, not a finished list.

Next, check each promising phrase in a free tool, Google's Keyword Planner or Google Trends both work, to see whether anyone is actually searching it and roughly how much competition exists. Then Google the phrase yourself and look at who currently ranks, if it's all national brands or big directories, that term is harder than it looks. Shortlist 15 to 20 phrases you could realistically win, and write one page or blog post around each over the coming weeks.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Do I need a paid tool like Ahrefs or Semrush to do this?

No. ChatGPT or Claude for brainstorming plus Google's free Keyword Planner or Google Trends is enough for a launch-stage business. Paid tools only earn their cost once you're publishing content regularly.

How many keywords should I target before launch?

Fifteen to twenty long-tail phrases is plenty. Pick a handful you can realistically build a page or post around this month rather than a long list you'll never action.

Won't AI just make up the search volumes?

Treat anything a chatbot tells you about volume or difficulty as a guess. Always confirm real numbers in a keyword tool before you commit content to a phrase.

Is this worth doing if most of my customers come from referrals or social media?

Yes, because search keeps working while you sleep, and it costs nothing to claim these long-tail phrases now, before a competitor who launches after you does it first.

Why this matters for you

A small business can't outspend a national chain on broad keywords or paid ads, and doesn't need to. Long-tail search is the one channel where a specific, well-targeted answer beats a bigger marketing budget, and AI removes the only real barrier to doing it properly, the hour of brainstorming most founders never get around to.
