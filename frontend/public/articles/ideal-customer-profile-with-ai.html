Why "Everyone" Is Actually Nobody

A vague audience doesn't widen your market. It just slows every decision after it.

Trying to speak to everyone who might want this is why so many founders write copy that sounds like nobody in particular. It's not laziness, it's fear of narrowing down. But a vague audience makes every later decision slower and pricier, which ad platform, which headline, which objection to answer first. An ICP ends the guessing.

Take a founder prepping a mobile dog-grooming van. Her pitch deck said her customer was "dog owners." Ten minutes of ICP prompts in ChatGPT later: working professionals aged 30-50, suburban postcodes, two dogs average, already paying £40+ a month for grooming, frustrated booking a slot around their commute. Same business, completely different ad copy and homepage headline, and she finally knew which local Facebook groups to post in.

Build Yours With AI in One Sitting

Four prompts, not a blank template.

First, describe your business idea to ChatGPT or Claude along with who you currently imagine buying it, as specific as you can, even guessing. Second, ask it directly: "what assumptions am I making about this audience that could be wrong?" Third, ask for three contrasting profiles split by budget, urgency, and how they decide, then pick the one with the sharpest pain point and money to spend now. Fourth, turn that profile into a one-page brief: demographics, daily frustrations, where they spend time online, likely objections, and the exact words they'd use for their problem.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Isn't an ideal customer profile the same as a buyer persona?

Close, but not quite. An ICP names the type of customer worth targeting, industry, budget, urgency. A persona adds a name and personality on top. Build the ICP first; add persona detail once real customers exist.

What if my business has more than one type of customer?

Normal, most small businesses end up with two or three. Ask the AI to rank them by willingness to pay and speed to decide, then build your first campaign around the top one.

Can AI really know my market without any real data?

No, treat its first draft as an educated guess, not research. It's a fast, structured starting point. Validate the sharpest assumptions with five real conversations before spending any ad budget.

How often should I revisit my ICP?

After your first 10 paying customers, then every six months. Real sales data corrects an AI's guesses faster than more prompting ever will.

Why this matters for you

For a one- or two-person business, an ICP isn't a pitch-deck slide. It's what stops you spending a limited ad budget and website copy on a message nobody specific recognises as written for them. Get this right before launch, and every later call, which platform, what to write in a FAQ, who to email first, gets faster because you've stopped guessing.
