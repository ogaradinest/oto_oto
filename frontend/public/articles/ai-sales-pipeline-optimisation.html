Why forecast accuracy is improving overall but still weak at the top

71% median, but only 7% break 90%

Median B2B pipeline forecasting accuracy has risen to 71% in 2026, a meaningful improvement from 54% in 2024, but only 7% of sales organisations achieve 90% accuracy or higher. Businesses using CRM systems properly see a 42% increase in forecast accuracy compared with those that do not, and adopting predictive analytics can improve accuracy by a further 30 to 40% over manual methods, yet the median result still falls well short of what most sales leaders would consider reliable.

The average B2B win rate has moved in the wrong direction over the same period, dropping from a 31 to 40% bracket down to 21 to 25%, meaning even businesses with reasonably accurate pipeline visibility are converting a smaller share of that pipeline into closed deals than a year earlier.

What actually causes most forecast errors

It is rarely the algorithm

Manual data entry drives roughly 70% of forecast errors, and forecast accuracy under 75% almost always traces back to data hygiene problems: stale contacts, missing decision-makers, and close dates a rep has quietly pushed back rather than admitted a deal has stalled. AI agents that automate data capture directly from calls, emails and calendar activity reach 90 to 95% accuracy on the underlying data, compared with the errors that accumulate when a rep manually updates a deal stage once a week from memory.

This matters because it changes where the real opportunity sits for most businesses. The gap between a 71% median forecast and a 90%-plus accuracy figure is more often a data quality gap than a modelling sophistication gap, and no forecasting algorithm, however advanced, produces an accurate prediction from stale or incomplete inputs.

Why a small business often does not need a new forecasting tool at all

Native CRM forecasting is usually good enough below £10 million in revenue

For most sales teams under roughly £10 million in annual revenue, the forecasting features already built into a standard CRM are good enough, and the money better spent is on data quality rather than a separate, dedicated forecasting layer. Deal scoring tools that assign each opportunity a score based on how closely it matches previously won deals, updating automatically as new activity is logged, are a genuinely useful addition, but only once the underlying data feeding them is reliable.

1 in 4 small and medium-sized businesses still manage contacts in spreadsheets rather than a CRM at all, which means for a meaningful share of small businesses, the actual first step toward better forecasting is not an AI feature of any kind, it is moving pipeline data into a system that can be relied upon and kept current.

A realistic order of operations for a small sales team

Data hygiene first, deal scoring second, a dedicated tool third if ever

Start by auditing your existing CRM data for the specific problems that drive 70% of forecast errors: stale contacts, missing decision-maker information, and close dates that have not moved in a suspiciously long time. Fixing this costs nothing beyond staff time and typically produces a larger accuracy improvement than any new tool would.

Once your CRM data is genuinely current, turn on or add deal scoring if your CRM supports it, since this is where AI meaningfully improves on a rep's gut feeling about which deals will actually close. Only consider a dedicated, separate forecasting platform once you are consistently generating over £10 million in annual revenue or have outgrown what your native CRM tools can do, since most teams below that threshold see a better return from cleaner data than a more sophisticated model.

The questions small business owners ask most

How accurate is B2B sales forecasting typically?

What actually causes most forecast inaccuracy?

Manual data entry problems account for roughly 70% of forecast errors: stale contacts, missing decision-makers, and close dates that have not been honestly updated, rather than a limitation in the forecasting method itself.

Does a small business need a dedicated AI forecasting tool?

Usually not below around £10 million in annual revenue. Native CRM forecasting features are typically good enough, and the money is better spent improving data quality than adding a separate forecasting layer.

What is deal scoring and when is it worth using?

A feature that assigns each open deal a score based on how closely it matches previously won deals, updating as new activity is logged. It is genuinely useful, but only once the underlying CRM data feeding it is accurate and current.

Why this matters for you

A small sales team often assumes a forecasting accuracy problem needs a new AI tool, when the data shows 70% of forecast errors trace back to manual data entry issues a new tool will not fix on its own.
