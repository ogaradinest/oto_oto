Where the 'too expensive' myth comes from

Enterprise headlines set the wrong benchmark

Big companies do spend big on AI: seven-figure rollouts, dedicated data teams, months of integration work. Those stories get the headlines, so it's easy to assume that's just what AI costs. But that spend buys custom infrastructure built for thousands of employees — not something a five-person business needs.

The barrier data tells a different story. In a survey of over 1,000 UK IT decision-makers, small businesses ranked lack of expertise (27%) above high costs (22%) as their biggest obstacle to AI adoption. The problem isn't the invoice. It's not knowing which tool solves which problem.

What small businesses are actually spending

Free trials, bundled tools, and £20–£30-a-month subscriptions

Take a six-person joinery firm outside Milton Keynes. The owner assumed AI meant a developer on retainer. Instead, she started with Copilot, already bundled into the Microsoft 365 subscription she was paying for anyway, plus a two-week free trial of an AI quoting tool. New spend in month one: zero.

Within six weeks, quote turnaround dropped from three days to same-day, and she picked up two extra jobs a month she'd previously lost to faster-quoting rivals. Most dedicated small-business AI tools settle into the £20–£30 a month range once a free trial ends — roughly one billable hour.

The ones who start small are the ones seeing gains

Scale isn't the deciding factor — starting is

Nearly three in ten UK businesses were using at least one AI technology by June 2026, per the ONS — this is no longer an enterprise-only experiment. Among small firms that have adopted AI, 59% report productivity gains and 24% report higher revenue, per the FSB.

None of that required a business the size of a bank. It required picking one repetitive task — quoting, scheduling, note-taking — and trying the cheapest tool that handles it, before deciding whether to spend more.
