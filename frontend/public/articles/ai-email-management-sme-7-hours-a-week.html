Marcus runs a six-person recruitment agency in Milton Keynes, and for three years his inbox has basically run him. Candidate replies at 9am, client updates at noon, a compliance query at 4pm, then another hour after dinner just trying to get back to zero — except zero never arrived. He wasn't ignoring anyone; there simply wasn't enough day. Twice last year a strong candidate went quiet because Marcus's reply landed two days after a rival agency's did, and one of those was a placement worth roughly £4,200 in fees. The inbox wasn't just eating his evenings — it was costing him money.

Knowledge workers lose 28% of the working week to email, per McKinsey Global Institute research — for a small agency without a dedicated ops person, that time comes straight out of billable hours.

The Inbox That Ran the Agency

Where Marcus's hours were actually going

Marcus isn't unusual for a small agency owner. Recruitment runs on email — candidate screening, client updates, compliance paperwork — and none of it waits politely for a quiet afternoon. He was spending close to two hours a day just triaging: deciding what was urgent, drafting near-identical replies to candidates asking about the same role, and re-reading client threads to remember what had already been promised.

The real cost showed up in what didn't get answered. A candidate who'd interviewed well but hadn't heard back in 48 hours accepted an offer elsewhere — a placement that would have been worth roughly £4,200 in fee income. It wasn't a one-off. Over a year, Marcus reckons slow replies cost the agency two or three placements it should have won.

What Changed When AI Took the First Pass

Handing drafting and sorting to an AI assistant

In the spring, Marcus connected an AI email assistant — he uses Fyxer, though Superhuman and Gmail's own Gemini features do similar jobs — to the agency's shared inbox. It sorts out spam and low-priority newsletters automatically, and drafts a reply in Marcus's tone for anything that looks like a candidate or client email. He just reviews, edits if needed, and sends.

The habit that stuck: Marcus now clears his real inbox in under 30 minutes most mornings instead of ninety, because the AI has already drafted the boring 80% — 'thanks for applying, here's the next step' style replies — and flagged the handful that actually need his judgement. He estimates the agency now saves around 7 hours a week across the team, and no candidate has gone more than a day without a reply since.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Is it safe to let an AI draft replies to candidates and clients?

Yes, as long as a human reviews before sending — most AI email tools draft rather than auto-send, so you keep control over anything sensitive like an offer or a rejection.

What does an AI email assistant cost for a small team?

Tools like Fyxer typically start around £20-£25 a month per user — modest next to the cost of a missed placement or client.

Will it work with Gmail or Outlook, or do we need to switch?

Most AI email assistants plug directly into Gmail or Outlook as an add-on, so there's no need to migrate your inbox or contacts.

Does it replace hiring an office admin?

Not entirely — it handles drafting and sorting, not judgement calls like negotiating terms. For a very small team it can delay that hire, though, rather than replace it.

Why this matters for you

For a business Marcus's size, there's no ops manager to absorb an overflowing inbox — every hour spent triaging email is an hour not spent placing candidates or managing client relationships, and every slow reply is a live risk of losing business to a faster competitor. McKinsey's research suggests that 28% of the working week disappearing into email isn't unusual, but for a six-person agency that's roughly a full extra working day, every week, that nobody budgeted for. Recovering even half of that through AI-assisted drafting is the difference between chasing admin and chasing revenue.
