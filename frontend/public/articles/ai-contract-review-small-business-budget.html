What went wrong when lawyers trusted a chatbot

The Ayinde and Al-Haroun warning

In June 2025, the Divisional Court ruled on two cases where lawyers had relied on an AI chatbot for legal research without checking its output, and the AI had invented cases that did not exist. The court was blunt: a general-purpose language model is not capable of conducting reliable legal research on its own, and lawyers who submit unverified AI output risk being held in contempt of court.

The lesson for a small business is not to avoid AI contract review. It is to understand what a general chatbot is actually good at, summarising and explaining, versus what it is not reliable at doing unsupervised, spotting every risk in a legal document with no second check.

What the SRA actually expects, even if you're not a law firm

Verify, disclose, stay accountable

The SRA's compliance guidance, updated 9 February 2026, does not ban AI. It requires solicitors using it to verify every output, protect client confidentiality, and remain personally responsible for the final result. A business without in-house counsel is not bound by SRA rules directly, but the same three habits, verify, protect sensitive information, stay accountable for the final decision, are exactly what keeps AI-assisted contract review safe for anyone using it.

In practice, this means never pasting an unsigned commercial contract with real counterparties, prices and terms into a general AI tool without checking the flagged risks against your own reading of the document, and never treating an AI summary as the final word on anything that carries real financial exposure.

What actually costs money right now

The gap between enterprise and nothing

Repeated and avoidable contract work costs UK businesses an estimated £12.2 to £12.6 billion a year, split roughly evenly between external law firms and in-house teams re-doing work that better first-pass review would have caught. Traditional legal review is not cheap either: top firm rates now average £449 an hour, and the average unresolved legal issue costs an SME £13,812.

Purpose-built AI contract review tools aimed at small businesses now sit well under that cost, from free tiers offering a handful of reviews a month to paid plans in the £15 to £80 a month range, alongside fixed-fee human review services from providers such as LawBite starting around £165 plus VAT for a one-off contract check.

A workable process with no in-house counsel

Layer the tools, do not replace judgement with them

Use an AI contract review tool as the first pass on any routine agreement: an NDA, a standard supplier terms document, a straightforward service agreement. Have it flag missing clauses, unusual liability terms and deviations from what you would normally accept. Read the flagged items yourself against the actual contract before acting on any of them.

Reserve a fixed-fee human review, from a solicitor or a service like LawBite, for anything genuinely high-value or unfamiliar: a lease, a partnership agreement, anything with real financial exposure if a clause turns out to matter. The AI tool handles volume cheaply. The fixed-fee human review handles the contracts where being wrong actually costs you.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Is it safe to use ChatGPT to review a contract?

For a first pass on a low-stakes document, it can help you spot obvious issues. A June 2025 court ruling found general AI chatbots are not reliable for legal research on their own, so treat its output as a starting point to check, rather than a final answer, especially on anything with real financial exposure.

What should a small business actually spend on AI contract review?

Purpose-built tools for small businesses typically run from free tiers to roughly £15 to £80 a month, a fraction of the £40,000-plus enterprise platforms built for legal teams reviewing thousands of contracts a year.

When should we still pay for a solicitor instead of using AI alone?

For anything with significant financial exposure or unfamiliar terms, a lease, a partnership agreement, a contract with an unusually large counterparty. Fixed-fee contract review services exist from as little as £165 plus VAT for exactly this situation.

Do SRA rules on AI use apply to us if we are not a law firm?

Not directly, but the same principles keep you safe: verify AI output before acting on it, think carefully about confidentiality before entering sensitive commercial details into a general AI tool, and keep a human accountable for the final decision.

Why this matters for you

A business with no in-house counsel cannot justify a £40,000 enterprise contract review platform, but it also cannot afford to keep losing money to the same missed clause twice, part of the £12.2 billion UK businesses lose annually to repeated contract mistakes. A cheap AI tool for routine agreements, backed by an occasional fixed-fee solicitor review for anything genuinely high-value, closes most of that gap without a retainer.
