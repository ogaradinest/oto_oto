Resolution Rate Is Not Deflection Rate

Two different numbers, often confused on purpose

Industry benchmarking puts the average AI resolution rate at 76%, with top performers reaching 80 to 84%. That sounds close to fully automated support. But separate research from Lorikeet found that only 14% of customer issues are currently resolved through genuine self-service, meaning a large share of what gets counted as 'resolved' still involves a human somewhere in the loop, reviewing, approving, or finishing the job.

Before trusting any resolution-rate figure a vendor quotes you, ask them to define it. A conversation that ends because the customer gave up is not the same as one that ended because the problem was actually solved.

The Real Cost Comparison

Per-conversation economics, not headline percentages

The clearest, most defensible number in this space is cost per conversation. Human-handled conversations typically run $6 to $12 each, largely driven by agent time. AI-handled conversations run $0.99 to $2.00. That gap, multiplied across thousands of monthly conversations, is where the genuine business case sits, not in vague productivity claims.

Realistic net cost reduction in the first year lands around 20 to 35%, once you account for the conversations that still need human review, the cost of the platform itself, and the time spent training and correcting the system early on. Vendor claims of 60 to 80% reduction usually describe a mature, fully tuned deployment, not what a business sees in month one.

What businesses that get this right actually see

Across surveyed businesses that deployed AI customer service tools, 74% report achieving positive ROI within six months. First-year average returns sit around 41%, rising past 124% by year three as the system improves and the team learns to configure it properly. Leading organisations, generally those with clean data and a well-scoped starting use case, report returns up to 8x their investment.

The pattern across almost every case worth reading about is the same: return improves sharply after the first few months, once the obvious edge cases have been fixed and staff trust the system enough to stop double-checking every response.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Is a 76% resolution rate realistic for a small business?

It's realistic for a narrow, well-defined use case, such as order status or booking changes. Resolution rates drop sharply for broad, open-ended queries, so scope matters more than the headline percentage.

How is 'resolved' actually being measured?

Always ask. Some vendors count a conversation as resolved if the customer doesn't reply again within a set window, which isn't the same as the problem actually being fixed.

When should we expect to see a return?

Most businesses that see success report positive ROI within six months, with returns improving substantially by year three as the system and the team both get better at using it.

Does cheaper per-conversation cost mean lower quality?

Not inherently, but it does mean the easy, repetitive queries get automated first. Complex or emotionally sensitive conversations should stay routed to a human regardless of what the cost comparison suggests.

Why this matters for you

Small businesses don't have the conversation volume to absorb a vague, poorly scoped AI deployment the way a call centre with thousands of daily tickets can. If you're fielding a few hundred customer enquiries a month, the per-conversation cost comparison above is the number that actually matters to you, not the headline resolution-rate percentage. Start with your single most repetitive enquiry type and measure it properly before expanding.
