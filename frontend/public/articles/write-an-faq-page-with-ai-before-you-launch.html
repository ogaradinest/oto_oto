Why founders need an FAQ page before day one

It's not a nice-to-have, it's a queue you're avoiding

Every question you answer by hand today is one you'll answer again next week. A new business gets asked the same handful of things on loop, pricing, delivery, returns, what actually makes you different. Stack those up over a month and you've quietly built yourself an unpaid, part-time customer service job before you've sold a single unit.

Take a founder setting up an online plant subscription box. In the first fortnight of her waitlist she had 40 sign-ups and 22 near-identical DMs asking about delivery windows and whether she shipped to Northern Ireland, close to four hours of typing the same three answers. A one-page FAQ, drafted with AI and edited into her own voice, cut that to almost nothing for the next intake.

How to build one with AI in about an hour

Five steps, no design skills required

Start by listing every question you've actually been asked, in group chats, waitlist replies, comments, wherever they've shown up. Fifteen to twenty is plenty. Feed that list to ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini with two or three sentences about your business, what you sell, who it's for, how delivery or booking works, and ask for plain-English draft answers.

Read every answer before it goes near your website. AI will guess at policies you haven't set yet, like your returns window or which countries you ship to, so correct anything invented and drop in your real numbers. Group the questions under headers like Ordering, Delivery, Returns, and About Us, then paste them into your website builder's FAQ or accordion block, most no-code tools, including Wix, Squarespace, and Durable, already have one.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Do I need paid software to build an FAQ page, or can I use a free AI tool?

A free tool is enough. ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini's free tiers can draft the whole page, a paid plan only matters if you want it kept updated automatically.

How long should a launch-stage FAQ page be?

Ten to fifteen questions is plenty for day one. Add more once real customers start asking things you didn't think to anticipate.

Should I publish AI's answers exactly as written?

No, always edit for accuracy first. AI doesn't know your actual return window or delivery cut-off times, it will invent something plausible if you don't supply the real detail yourself.

Does an FAQ page actually help with Google search, or is it just for customers?

Both. Pages marked up with FAQ schema can appear directly inside Google results, a small visibility boost before your site has any real search history.

Why this matters for you

For a solo founder or a two-person team, every hour spent typing the same answer by hand is an hour not spent on sourcing, selling, or building the actual product.
