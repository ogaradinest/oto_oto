Where the real risk actually lives

It's the free personal account, not the technology

Free, personal versions of ChatGPT can use your conversations to improve their models unless you manually turn that off. That's the version most people try first, and often the one staff quietly keep using — sometimes with a client's contract details pasted straight in.

Business tiers work differently. ChatGPT Business/Enterprise, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Claude for Work all state, in their own documentation, that customer prompts are excluded from model training by default — your data isn't used to improve someone else's product unless you opt in.

A 12-person marketing agency near Milton Keynes hit this last year: an account manager was using a personal ChatGPT login for client campaign briefs, budgets and contacts included. Moving the team to a paid Team plan cost about £22 per user a month, and took every brief out of a free, trainable account.

What actually needs a policy

Three rules cover most of the risk

You don't need a governance committee to close this gap. Three things do most of the work: use paid business-tier accounts, not personal logins, for anything touching customer or financial data; turn off model training in settings as a second safeguard even on a paid plan; and write one page naming which tools are approved and which data (health records, payment details) never goes in at all.

The ICO's own AI and Data Protection guidance points the same way — for most small businesses this is about proportionate controls, not a legal department. Its AI and Data Protection Risk Toolkit is built for a non-specialist to work through directly.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Is it illegal to put customer data into ChatGPT?

Not automatically, but it can breach GDPR without a lawful basis, and if the tool retains or trains on it in ways you haven't accounted for. Business-tier plans with training off remove most of that risk.

What's the real difference between a free and paid AI account?

Free personal accounts often train on your input by default. Paid business plans exclude your data from training and add admin controls and retention settings.

Do I need a lawyer to write an AI usage policy?

No. A one-page note naming approved tools and banned data categories covers most of the risk.

Does using AI tools mean I need a Data Protection Officer?

Not for most small businesses — you need a lawful basis and a record of what you're doing, but a dedicated DPO only kicks in past certain size thresholds.

Why this matters for you

Small businesses handle exactly the data that makes this feel high-stakes — customer contacts, invoices, HR records — without the IT security team a large enterprise has to catch a mistake first. The fix has to be simple for that reason: no governance department, just moving everyone off free personal logins onto one paid, training-off account in an afternoon.
