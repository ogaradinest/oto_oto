Governance Already Applies to You

The rules didn't wait for you to have a compliance team

UKSI 2026/425, the regulation behind the ICO's new code of practice on AI and automated decision-making, came into force on 12 May 2026. The ICO's draft guidance on automated decision-making closed for consultation on 29 May 2026, with final guidance expected over summer 2026 and a full statutory code following in 2027.

None of that requires a legal team to act on. Between 35% and 39% of UK SMEs are already using AI-powered tools, up from around 25% in 2024, and most of that use already touches customer data in some way: a chatbot answering enquiries, a tool scoring leads, software flagging which invoices to chase first. If AI is shaping a decision about a real customer, the direction of travel from regulators is clear enough to act on now.

Quick Answers

What small business owners ask about AI governance

Do the new ICO rules actually apply to a small business?

Yes. UKSI 2026/425 and the ICO's guidance on automated decision-making apply based on what the AI does, not how large the business is. If a tool influences a decision about a customer, the rules are relevant.

Is UK GDPR still the main thing to worry about?

It's the foundation. The new AI-specific code sits alongside UK GDPR and the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 rather than replacing them. If you're already handling customer data responsibly, you're most of the way there.

What does "named owner" actually mean in practice?

One real person who knows a given AI tool exists, knows roughly what it does, and would be the first person you'd ask if it did something wrong. Not a title. An actual name.

What's the very first thing to do?

List every AI tool in use across the business, who owns each one, what data goes in, what comes out, and whether it connects to any other system. That single list underpins all four documents above.

Why this matters for you

You don't need to match an enterprise compliance function, and trying to would waste time you don't have. What you do need is enough written down that if a customer ever asks "why did your system decide that about me," someone in your business can give a real answer instead of a shrug.
