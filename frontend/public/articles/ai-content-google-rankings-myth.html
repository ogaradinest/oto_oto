Where the fear actually comes from

A pile of horror stories, not a documented policy

The panic usually traces back to real traffic drops after a Google update — but those hit content mills publishing hundreds of thin, unedited pages a month, not AI use itself. A two-person accountancy firm that published forty AI-drafted posts in a month, with nobody reading them first, saw traffic fall within eight weeks. A similar firm publishing eight AI-drafted posts a month, each with twenty minutes of real editing and one specific client example added, saw organic traffic grow by roughly 30% over the same stretch. Same tool — the difference was whether a human touched the draft first.

What Google actually checks for

Quality and oversight, not who typed it

Google's own guidance says appropriate use of AI isn't against its rules, so long as content isn't produced primarily to manipulate rankings. Its 2025 Quality Rater Guidelines go further: AI content is rated "Lowest" quality only when it lacks human oversight and review, not because AI wrote the first draft. What actually separates a page that ranks are details no AI can invent alone — your real prices, your actual turnaround times, a judgment call from a job you did last month. That editing gap, not the tool, is what most small businesses skip.

The real risk worth watching in 2026

AI Overviews, not AI penalties

The real 2026 shift isn't a penalty on AI-written pages — it's Google's own AI Overviews sitting above results and quietly taking the click. Ahrefs found an AI Overview correlates with a 58% lower click-through rate for whoever ranks first, regardless of who wrote that page. The fix isn't writing less with AI or more without it — it's making sure whatever you publish is specific enough to your business to earn the click or citation, instead of getting summarised and skipped.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Will Google penalise my site for using AI to write blog posts?

No — Google says it doesn't check how content was made, only whether it's genuinely useful. It penalises content published purely to manipulate rankings, AI-written or not.

So I can publish AI drafts without reading them first?

No. Google's Quality Rater Guidelines rate AI content "Lowest" specifically when it lacks human oversight — a quick edit and a real example is what matters.

What should I actually worry about in 2026 SEO?

AI Overviews above the results, which cut click-through for the top result by around 58% regardless of who wrote it. Write specifically enough to still earn the click.

How much editing does an AI draft really need?

Enough that a customer would recognise it as coming from your business — one real example, checked facts, and cutting anything generic enough to be any competitor's.

Why this matters for you

Most small businesses have no in-house content team, so AI drafting is often the only realistic way to publish consistently. Treating every AI post as radioactive means falling behind; treating it as a free pass to publish unedited means genuinely earning the traffic drop people fear. The middle ground — draft with AI, edit with a person, add one thing only your business could say — costs minutes.
