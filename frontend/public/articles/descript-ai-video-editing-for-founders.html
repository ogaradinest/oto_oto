It edits video like a Word document

Delete the text, delete the clip

Descript transcribes whatever you film or record, then lets you edit the transcript instead of a timeline. Cut a sentence out of the text and the matching video or audio disappears with it. Say "um" four times in a row? Highlight it, hit delete, gone. There's no scrubbing back and forth trying to find the exact frame.

It also has a filler-word remover that strips out most of the "um", "uh" and dead air automatically, an AI voice tool (Overdub) that can clone your own voice from a short recording so you can fix a flubbed line without re-filming, and a screen-recorder built in for product walkthroughs. None of it requires knowing what a keyframe is.

Quick answers

The questions most founders ask first

Do I need any video editing experience to use Descript?

No. If you can delete text in a document, you can cut a video in Descript. The interface is built around the transcript, not a timeline.

Is the AI voice clone (Overdub) actually usable for real marketing videos?

It's good enough for fixing a single flubbed line or a name you mispronounced, not for narrating a whole video from a script. You train it on your own voice with your consent, and most founders use it sparingly rather than as the primary voice.

What's the cheapest way to try it before committing?

Start on the free plan. It caps you on transcription minutes and export quality, but it's enough to edit two or three short founder-update or product clips and see whether the workflow actually saves you time.

Will using AI-edited video make my brand look less genuine?

The data says no: 74% of consumers said in 2026 they were open to receiving AI-assisted video from brands, up from 65% the year before. The editing is AI-assisted, but it's still your face and your words, which is what audiences actually respond to.
