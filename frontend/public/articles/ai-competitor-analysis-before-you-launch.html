Ask for a map, not a list

The prompt matters more than the tool

The lazy prompt — "who are my competitors?" — gets you a shallow list of names. A better one gets you a structured comparison: "Identify the likely top five competitors for a [type of business] in [your area/market]. Compare their pricing, positioning, and what customers complain about in reviews, then suggest where I could position differently." Ask it to cite where it's getting each claim from, so you know what to double-check yourself.

A founder planning a mobile car-valeting round in Milton Keynes did exactly this. The AI surfaced four local competitors she hadn't found by searching alone, including one running an app-based booking system at £35 a wash against her planned £45. That one output changed her pricing and pushed her to lead with a loyalty scheme instead of competing on price alone — a repositioning she made before spending a penny on kit.

Who are they? What do they charge? What do reviews complain about? Where's the gap?

Verify what AI can't see

It doesn't know today's prices or who's actually still trading

AI models are confident even when their information is stale or invented, and they can't see live pricing pages, current Google reviews, or whether a competitor has quietly shut down. After the AI gives you a starting map, spend thirty minutes checking it: search each competitor on Google and Companies House, read their most recent reviews, and check their actual current prices. Free tools like Similarweb's traffic estimates or a quick look at their social media posting frequency tell you who's actually active versus who just has a website.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Which AI tool should I use for this?

ChatGPT, Claude, or Perplexity all work. Perplexity is useful because it searches the live web and shows sources, which matters for a task built on checking facts.

How long should a first pass actually take?

Budget ninety minutes: about an hour for the AI-assisted map and prompting, then thirty minutes verifying the key claims yourself before you trust any of it.

Can AI tell me if a gap in the market is real?

It can spot a gap in what's being said or priced, but it can't confirm real demand. Treat any gap it finds as a hypothesis to test with a handful of real conversations, not a fact.

Do I need to pay for a research tool?

No. A free ChatGPT or Claude account plus thirty minutes of manual checking on Google, Companies House, and review sites covers a solid first pass for most small local businesses.

Why this matters for you

For a small, local, or first-time business, competitor research isn't a nice-to-have slide in a pitch deck — it's the difference between guessing a price and knowing one. Skipping it doesn't make the competition disappear; it just means you find out about it from a customer who chose someone cheaper, instead of from a spreadsheet you built in an afternoon for free.
