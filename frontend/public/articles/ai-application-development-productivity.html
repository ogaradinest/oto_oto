The productivity numbers are real, and verified

Not vendor marketing, independent measurement

The GitHub and Accenture study is one of the more rigorous pieces of evidence in this space: 4,800 developers, measured task completion, and a clear before-and-after comparison. The 55% faster completion time and the drop in pull request turnaround from 9.6 to 2.4 days both come from that measurement, not from marketing copy.

Gartner's own research corroborates the direction, if not the exact number: 90% of engineering leaders report productivity improvements from AI coding tools, with a net average gain of 19.3% once you account for the time spent reviewing and correcting AI-generated code. Across a composite of major tools, the average developer saves roughly 9.4 hours a week.

Where the money is actually going

A fast-growing slice of enterprise software spend

AI-assisted coding is now one of the fastest-growing categories of enterprise software spend. Coding agents and copilots represent roughly $4 billion of the broader $19 billion 2025 application-layer spend, around 55% of departmental AI budgets going toward coding tools specifically. Three products, GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, and Cursor, have each independently crossed $1 billion in annualised revenue, which tells you this isn't a niche experiment anymore.

Developer adoption backs this up. Stack Overflow's 2025 survey of more than 49,000 developers found 84% now use or plan to use AI coding tools, with 51% using them daily. If your development partner isn't using these tools yet, that's worth asking about directly.

What this means when you commission custom software

Faster delivery, but the same scoping discipline matters more, not less

Faster coding does not mean faster scoping, faster design decisions, or faster testing, and those remain the parts of a project most likely to slip. If anything, AI-accelerated development raises the stakes on getting requirements right upfront, because a team that can write code 55% faster can also build the wrong thing 55% faster.

A reasonable question to ask any development partner in 2026 is not whether they use AI tools, most competent ones do, but how they've adjusted their scoping and review process to match the new speed. A team still running an old-style review process on AI-accelerated code is likely to ship faster and worse.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Should this change what I expect to pay for custom software?

It's reasonable to expect faster delivery on well-scoped work, though hourly or day rates haven't necessarily dropped in proportion to the speed gain. Ask any quote to reflect the new delivery speed, not just the old one.

Are AI coding tools actually reliable, or just fast?

Both, within limits. Gartner's 19.3% net productivity figure already accounts for review and correction time, meaning the gains are real even after quality checks, not before them.

Does this apply to small, simple projects too?

Yes, arguably more so. Smaller, well-defined builds are exactly where AI coding tools perform most reliably, since there's less ambiguous legacy context to get wrong.

What should I ask a development agency about their AI tool use?

Ask which tools they use, and more importantly, what their review process looks like for AI-generated code. A confident, specific answer is a good sign. A vague one is worth probing further.

Why this matters for you

If you've been quoted a development timeline that feels like it's from a few years ago, it might genuinely be out of date. Development teams using current AI coding tools are measurably faster, which should show up in your quote and your delivery date. It doesn't mean cutting corners on scoping, if anything, clear requirements matter more when the build phase moves this quickly.
