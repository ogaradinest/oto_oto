The prediction and the current reality are on different timelines

What Gartner's own two surveys show side by side

Gartner's 2029 prediction of 80% autonomous resolution is a genuine forecast from a credible source, not marketing copy. It is also four years away and describes an industry-wide direction, not a guarantee about any specific tool you might sign up for this quarter. Gartner's own October 2025 survey of active customer service leaders found a much more modest present-day picture: 20% have reduced staffing because of AI, and the majority, 55%, describe staffing as stable even as volumes rise.

The practical implication for a small business is straightforward: evaluate a tool on what it demonstrably does today, in a trial with your own customers, not on the multi-year roadmap a vendor's sales team describes during the pitch.

The lock-in question most small businesses never ask

What Zapier's own switching data shows

Zapier's 2026 survey found 81% of enterprise leaders worried about dependency on a single AI vendor, and 74% said losing that vendor would disrupt daily operations or leave them unable to function. The more revealing figure sits underneath those headlines: among leaders who had actually attempted to migrate away from an AI vendor, only 42% called the process smooth. Confidence about switching and the actual experience of switching are two different things.

Before signing with any AI customer service vendor, ask three specific questions: can you export your full conversation history and trained response library in a usable format, what is the minimum contract term, and what does the vendor's off-boarding process actually involve if you decide to leave. A vendor confident in their product will answer all three without hesitation.

The other question: what happens when it hands off to a human

This is usually what customers actually notice

Zendesk's 2026 customer experience research found 74% of consumers describe having to repeat themselves across interactions as very frustrating. That frustration is rarely about the AI's conversational ability itself. It is almost always about a poor handoff: the human agent who picks up the conversation has no visibility into what the customer already explained to the AI, so the customer starts over. Testing any AI customer service tool specifically on this handoff, not just on how well it answers questions alone, catches the failure mode that actually drives complaints.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Should a small business trust vendor predictions about future AI customer service capability?

Treat them as direction, not a guarantee for your contract term. Gartner's own October 2025 survey found only 20% of customer service leaders have actually cut staffing due to AI so far, well short of the industry's longer-range 2029 predictions. Evaluate a tool on a trial with your own customers, not a roadmap.

What is the biggest AI vendor lock-in risk for a small business?

Not being able to take your conversation history and trained responses with you. Zapier's 2026 survey found that among enterprise leaders who had actually tried switching AI vendors, only 42% described the process as smooth, despite most expecting it to be straightforward beforehand.

What should I check before signing a contract with an AI customer service vendor?

Ask whether you can export your full conversation history and response library, what the minimum contract term is, and exactly what the off-boarding process involves. These three questions catch most of the lock-in risk before it becomes a problem.

Why do customers get frustrated with AI customer service even when the AI answers correctly?

Usually because of a poor handoff to a human agent. Zendesk's research found 74% of consumers find repeating themselves across interactions very frustrating, which is a design and integration issue rather than a reflection of how good the underlying AI actually is.

Why this matters for you

A small business signing its first AI customer service contract is far less likely than a large enterprise to have a procurement team asking about data export rights or contract exit terms, which makes the Zapier lock-in findings more relevant here, not less. The fix is three specific questions asked before signing, not a legal review: can you export your data, how long is the minimum term, and what does leaving actually involve.

Try this next

Before signing with any AI customer service vendor, or before renewing an existing contract, email them three questions in writing: can we export our full conversation history and trained responses, what is the minimum contract term, and what does off-boarding actually look like if we leave. Keep the written answers on file, since a vague or evasive reply is itself useful information.
