Work Out What You're Actually Protecting

Not every chat needs an NDA, but the ones with your real edge do

An NDA only matters if there's something specific worth keeping quiet: a product mechanism, a pricing model, a customer list, source code, or a supplier deal you've spent months arranging. If you're just describing a general market gap, skip it; nobody can steal "an app for dog walkers".

Decide early whether you need a mutual NDA (both sides share something, like you and a potential co-founder) or a one-way NDA (only you're disclosing, like pitching to a freelance developer or supplier). Getting this wrong is the most common mistake Sprintlaw's own guide flags for UK founders drafting their first one.

Draft It With AI in the Time It Takes to Make Coffee

Give it specifics, not a vague request for "an NDA"

Tell ChatGPT or Claude exactly who's involved, what's confidential, how long it needs protecting (one to two years is standard for a pitch, longer for genuine trade secrets), and that you want England and Wales governing law. Ask for the mutual or one-way version you settled on above.

You'll have a usable first draft in minutes, not the days a solicitor's inbox usually takes. Read it back against your actual scenario: does it name the right parties, cover the right information, and say what happens to any files or notes once the conversation ends?

Quick Answers

The questions owners ask most

Do I really need a solicitor for a basic NDA?

Not for a standard pitch-stage NDA between two parties. An AI-drafted version covers the essentials fine. Save the solicitor's budget for anything involving serious IP, a large investment, or terms you don't fully understand.

Should my NDA be mutual or one-way?

Mutual if both sides are sharing confidential information, such as you and a potential co-founder. One-way if you're the only one disclosing, such as pitching your idea to a freelancer or supplier who isn't sharing anything back.

Will an AI-drafted NDA actually hold up if someone breaches it?

A clear, specific NDA with the right parties, scope, and duration is enforceable regardless of who drafted it. What makes NDAs fail in practice is vague wording, not the tool used to write them, so read the draft carefully before you rely on it.

What should never go in the confidential information, even with an NDA signed?

Anything you can't afford to lose if the agreement is ignored. An NDA gives you legal recourse after a breach; it doesn't stop someone from talking. For your single most sensitive detail, consider whether you need to share it at all before you've even launched.

Why this matters for you

For a founder who hasn't registered a company yet, let alone hired a solicitor, a £400 quote for a one-off NDA is exactly the kind of cost that gets postponed until "after launch", which is usually the point where it's too late to matter.
