Amir runs a two-van locksmith and emergency call-out business outside Milton Keynes, most of his day spent under a bonnet, up a ladder, or picking a lock at 11pm for someone standing in the rain. His phone rang constantly, and for years it rang straight to a voicemail nobody checked until the job was already done. A locked-out customer doesn't wait around. They ring the next name on Google, and Amir never even knew that job had existed.

69% of callers who reach a small business's voicemail hang up without leaving a message, and most never try again. An unanswered phone is effectively a closed shop.

The phone nobody was ever free to answer

Why the busiest hours were also the quietest on the phone

For Amir, the problem was never demand, it was timing. Emergency trades get calls exactly when they're least able to take them: mid-job, hands full, engine running. Every unanswered call rang out to a generic voicemail that didn't even name the business. Callers heard it and hung up. Moneypenny's small business research found a third of micro-businesses miss their incoming calls entirely, and most callers who land on voicemail never try again.

Add it up over a normal week for a one or two-van outfit: five or six missed calls, each worth £60 for a simple lockout to £300 for an after-hours emergency. Miss half of those and a competitor who picked up first is quietly taking £150 to £900 a week that was always Amir's to win. Federation of Small Businesses research puts the yearly cost of this to a typical UK trades business at around £24,000.

What changed when every call got answered

Same number, a different first response

Amir didn't hire a receptionist. He set up an AI phone-answering tool on his existing number instead. Every call is picked up in under two rings, gives the business name, asks what's needed and where, then books a straightforward job into his calendar or texts him a summary if it needs his judgement. He still sets the price, and anything urgent gets flagged to his phone immediately, the tool just makes sure nobody hits a dead voicemail again.

In the two months since switching it on, Amir reckons he's capturing roughly 8 to 10 jobs a month that used to slip through. At an average job value of £120, that's over £1,000 a month recovered from calls that were always coming in, just never answered. The tool costs less than the value of one missed callout.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Won't customers find it off-putting that AI answers the phone?

Most don't mind if it sounds natural and books them in fast. The alternative was silence, and a quick answer beats a voicemail every time.

What happens to calls that need a real person straight away?

Anything urgent gets flagged to the owner's phone with the details already taken. The tool handles routine bookings and hands over what it shouldn't decide alone.

How much does something like this cost?

Most AI call-answering tools run to the tens of pounds a month, well under the cost of one missed job in a typical week.

Is this only useful for trades and call-out businesses?

No. Any small business that loses bookings to an unanswered phone, salons, clinics, garages, letting agents, sees the same pattern: whoever answers first gets the job.

Why this matters for you

For a small business, a missed call is a sale that just walked to whichever competitor picked up. Hiring a receptionist rarely makes sense below a certain size, and a generic voicemail greeting quietly tells callers nobody's really there. An AI phone-answering tool sits in between: cheap enough for a one or two-person outfit, fast enough that a caller never gets time to look up the next name, and it works the same at 11pm as it does at 11am.
