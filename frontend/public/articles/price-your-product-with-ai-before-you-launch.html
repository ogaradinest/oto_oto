Why "what feels fair" is costing you

Guessing and copying both land you below your real floor

Take a founder launching a virtual assistant service for solo consultants. She picks £25 an hour because a friend in a similar business charges that, and it sounds reasonable, until she adds up her software subscriptions, the roughly 30% of her week that's admin and invoicing rather than billable work, and the tax she'll owe on top. Her real floor, the point where she's actually paying herself rather than quietly working for free, turns out to be closer to £42 an hour. She'd been undercharging by 40% without realising it.

That gap is exactly why over a third of small businesses say they have no plans to raise prices even as their own costs climb. The number they started with was never actually calculated, so raising it feels like a decision with no basis, rather than a correction.

How to build your number with AI in an afternoon

Three steps: cost floor, value check, competitor reality check

Start with the floor. List every real cost: subscriptions, materials, the tax you'll owe, and a realistic estimate of how much of your week is unbillable, admin, sales calls, invoicing, usually 30 to 40%. Feed all of it to ChatGPT or Claude and ask it to calculate the minimum you need to charge per hour or per unit to cover that with a margin. That turns "am I too expensive?" into a maths question with an actual answer.

Next, check value, not just cost. Describe what the result is worth to your customer, not just the hours it takes you, and ask the AI to sketch two or three pricing scenarios, hourly, package, tiered, based on that value. Then Google what three real competitors publicly charge for something comparable, and sanity-check the AI's suggestion against what the market will actually bear before you commit to a number.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Can I just ask ChatGPT what to charge?

Not directly, it doesn't know your market's willingness to pay. What it's genuinely good for is the maths: turning your real costs, tax, and unbillable time into an accurate floor, fast.

What should I actually type in to get useful numbers?

List your monthly costs, your target income, and your realistic billable hours or units, then ask it to calculate your break-even price and show the working. Vague prompts get vague numbers.

Do I need a dedicated AI pricing tool instead of a chatbot?

Not before launch. Tools like Prisync or Competera earn their subscription once you have real sales data to optimise against. A general chatbot is enough for your first number.

How often should I revisit the price once I've launched?

On a schedule, not in a panic. Quote new customers at the higher rate first, then apply a modest increase to existing ones roughly once a year.

Why this matters for you

For a small business, pricing is the only lever that improves margin with no extra delivery cost attached, a 10% price rise drops almost straight to profit, while a new client costs time and money to win. Getting the starting number right before launch matters even more, because every customer who signs up at an underpriced rate is one you'll either serve at a loss or have to awkwardly renegotiate with later.
