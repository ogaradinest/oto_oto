Why the Sticker Price Is Not the Real Price

Seats, usage fees, and add-ons

Most platforms now price on at least two dimensions: a per-seat monthly fee for human agents, and a separate usage-based charge for every AI-handled resolution. A team paying around £55 a seat a month for the base platform can still face an additional AI copilot add-on running another £50 a seat, before a single AI resolution is counted.

The per-resolution fee is where costs scale unpredictably for a growing business. A tool charging under $0.30 per resolution and one charging $2 per resolution look similar on a features page, but at real volume the gap between them is the difference between a manageable software line item and a number that needs board approval.

What 20,000 Resolutions a Month Actually Costs

The comparison that headline pricing hides

At 20,000 AI-handled resolutions a month, a mid-size team can expect to pay roughly $19,800 on a platform charging under a dollar per resolution, rising past $30,000 on a platform charging around $1.50 per resolution plus per-seat fees, and over $40,000 on a platform charging $2 per conversation plus a separate service-desk licence. Very few small businesses handle 20,000 resolutions a month, but the principle holds at any scale: request the per-resolution rate and any add-on fees in writing before comparing two vendors' entry-level prices, because the entry-level price rarely includes the AI feature you are actually buying the platform for.

The Real Comparison Point Is Your Current Staffing Cost

Compare against the alternative, not just other vendors

Before comparing AI customer service vendors against each other, compare the cheapest sensible AI option against what a part-time human hire actually costs: roughly £12 to £18 an hour in the UK in 2026. An AI tool handling the majority of first-contact queries at a fraction of that hourly cost is very likely worth adopting, almost regardless of which specific vendor is chosen, provided the resolution rate reflects genuine problem-solving rather than a deflection statistic.

The vendor comparison matters most once a business has already decided AI customer service is worth doing at all. At that point, the fine print, per-resolution pricing, add-on costs, contract minimums, is what actually determines whether the business case holds up after the first invoice.

A Simple Way to Shortlist Without a Lengthy Procurement Process

Three questions, not twelve demos

Ask every vendor the same three questions before a demo: what is the all-in cost per resolution at our expected volume, including any add-ons; what happens to the price if our volume doubles; and can we see the escalation and confidence-scoring features working live, not in a slide.

A vendor that cannot answer the first two questions clearly and in writing is not ready for a small business budget, regardless of how capable the underlying AI looks in a demo. Price transparency at your actual volume is a better predictor of a smooth relationship than any feature comparison chart.

Quick Answers

The questions owners ask most

Why do AI customer service platforms cost more than the advertised price?

Most charge a per-seat fee for the base platform plus a separate usage-based fee for every AI-handled resolution, and often an additional AI copilot add-on on top. These three rarely appear together on the homepage pricing table.

How do we compare vendors fairly?

Ask for the all-in cost per resolution at your actual expected monthly volume, in writing, from each vendor, rather than comparing their advertised starting prices, which rarely include the AI usage fee you are buying the platform for.

Is AI customer service worth it if we only handle a few hundred queries a month?

Usually, yes, if the alternative is part-time staff time costing £12 to £18 an hour. At low volume, per-resolution AI pricing stays cheap in absolute terms even before seat fees are considered.

What is the single most useful question to ask a vendor before signing?

What happens to our price if our volume doubles. Usage-based pricing can make a cheap-looking platform expensive quickly, and a vendor who cannot answer this clearly in writing is a warning sign.

Why this matters for you

A small business signing up for an AI customer service platform based on the advertised seat price alone can find the real bill considerably higher once usage fees and add-ons are included. Modelling the all-in cost at your actual volume, and comparing that number against the real cost of part-time staff time, gives an honest answer to whether the tool is worth it, rather than a comparison based on marketing pages.

Compare that single number against your current staffing cost per hour. That comparison, not a features list, is what should decide the purchase.
