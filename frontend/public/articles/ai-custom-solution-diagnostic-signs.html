At the same time, Gartner's separate research finds over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027, mostly from escalating costs, unclear business value, or inadequate risk controls. Custom is not automatically the wrong call. It is also not automatically the right one, and the gap between those two Gartner findings is exactly where a small business needs a clearer test than a gut feeling.

Check what your existing tools already do before assuming you need custom

The bar for 'off-the-shelf' keeps moving

Gartner's prediction that task-specific agents will reach 40% of enterprise applications by the end of 2026 means the software you already pay for is absorbing capability that used to require a bespoke build. A CRM, a helpdesk platform, or an accounting tool that lacked a specific automated feature a year ago may already have added it. Before requesting a custom quote for anything, check the changelog or release notes of the tools you already use from the last six months. It is a five-minute check that regularly rules out a project that would otherwise have cost thousands.

Three genuine signs you need something built

Not just 'the free tool is annoying'

The workflow spans three or more systems that were never designed to talk to each other, and no existing vendor bridges all of them: this is the clearest technical signal, since off-the-shelf tools are built for the two or three most common integrations, not every possible combination a specific business happens to run.

The task you want automated is specifically what makes your business different from a competitor, not a generic efficiency gain every business in your sector also wants. McKinsey's research on AI strategy frames this precisely: differentiate through proprietary, customisable capability where it creates genuine advantage, and use existing solutions everywhere else. If the task is not part of what makes a customer choose you over the business down the road, it rarely justifies a custom build.

You hold a genuinely large, proprietary dataset that a generic model has never seen and cannot use without specific training on it, such as years of your own inspection reports, claims history, or technical documentation. A generic tool with no access to that data will always underperform something built to use it directly.

The caution that comes with deciding to build

Over 40% of agentic projects get cancelled for a reason

Even once one of the three signs above genuinely applies, Gartner's finding that over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027, mostly from escalating costs and unclear business value, is worth treating as a warning rather than a statistic about other companies. The practical response is to scope a small, narrow pilot around the single clearest sign first, with one measurable outcome, rather than committing to a full custom build in one step. A project that proves itself on one narrow task is far easier to defend, and far cheaper to cancel if it does not work, than one scoped as a full transformation from the outset.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

How do I know if my business actually needs a custom AI solution?

Look for three specific signs: a workflow spanning multiple systems no vendor bridges, a task that is genuinely what differentiates your business rather than a generic efficiency gain, and a proprietary dataset a generic AI tool has never seen. If none of these clearly apply, an off-the-shelf tool is usually the better starting point.

Is it worth checking existing tools before commissioning a custom build?

Yes. Gartner predicts 40% of enterprise applications will have task-specific AI agents built in by the end of 2026, up from under 5% in 2025, so a feature that required a custom build eighteen months ago may already exist natively in a tool you already pay for.

What is 'agent washing' and why does it matter when evaluating vendors?

Agent washing is when a vendor rebrands an existing chatbot or automation tool as an AI agent without genuine agentic capability. Gartner estimates only around 130 of the thousands of vendors making this claim are real, which is worth checking before ruling out an off-the-shelf option.

Should a small business jump straight to a full custom AI build?

No. Gartner found over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by 2027, largely from escalating costs and unclear value. Scoping a small pilot around the single clearest need first, with one measurable outcome, is a lower-risk way to test whether a custom build is genuinely worth it.

Why this matters for you

A small business has the least room to absorb a cancelled custom project, which makes the gap between Gartner's two findings, more built-in AI in existing tools, but a high cancellation rate for agentic projects, more relevant here than at a large enterprise. Checking existing tools first and testing with a narrow pilot before committing to a full build protects a budget that cannot easily absorb an expensive, cancelled project.

Try this next

Before requesting a quote for any custom AI build, spend fifteen minutes checking the release notes of the tools you already use from the last six months, and write down which of the three signs, a multi-system gap, a genuine differentiator, or proprietary data, actually applies to what you want built. If none clearly apply, look harder at what already exists before commissioning anything new.
