UK law firms lose an average of 4.16 billable hours per fee earner every week to poor time recording, working out to nearly £2 million a year in unrecorded billing for a firm of 50 fee earners. For a small practice with five or six fee earners, the proportional loss is identical, and it happens for the same reason: billable time gets forgotten between the actual work and the moment someone sits down to record it, usually hours or days later.

AI time-capture tools do not bill more aggressively. They capture time that already happened and was simply never written down, an average of 28 extra billable minutes a day per fee earner in firms that have adopted them.

Where the lost time actually goes

It is not laziness, it is the gap between doing and recording

Time recording and billing account for 28% of the frustration fee earners report with their current systems, more than any other single cause, ahead of switching between disconnected applications and document drafting. The pattern is consistent: work gets done, the details are clear in the moment, and by the time someone opens the time-recording system at the end of the day, or the end of the week, the specifics have blurred and get rounded down or skipped entirely.

This problem hits a small firm harder in relative terms than a large one, even though it is common across firms of every size. A large firm's loss is a rounding error against total revenue. For a five or six fee-earner practice, 4.16 hours a week per person is a meaningful share of total billable capacity.

Why there is a real gap between what leaders believe and what actually happens

71% versus 71%

In one survey, 71% of firm leaders said they were confident their case management system allowed fee earners to capture all their billable time. The exact same proportion, 71%, of fee earners said the opposite. This gap matters because it means most firms are not aware they have a problem worth measuring, let alone fixing.

AI-powered time capture closes this gap by working passively in the background, logging activity, drafts, calls, document time, as it happens rather than relying on someone to remember and manually enter it hours later.

What this is actually worth, and what it costs

The payback point is lower than most firms assume

Firms adopting AI-powered time tracking report recovering roughly 28 additional billable minutes per fee earner per day, working out to an average of £28,000 per lawyer per year in previously missed billable work. AI invoice drafting and time-capture add-ons typically cost $30 to $50 per user per month on top of a base billing platform.

Industry pricing analysis suggests the investment pays back once a firm has more than 50 to 75 active matters or three or more fee earners, a threshold most small practices clear comfortably. Below that, the tool still helps, but the case for it rests more on accuracy and reduced admin stress than on a clean payback calculation.

Getting started without replacing your whole billing system

Layer the capability, do not rebuild the platform

Most modern legal billing platforms, Clio among them, now offer AI time-capture and invoice-drafting as an add-on to an existing subscription rather than requiring a full system migration. This is the sensible entry point for a small firm: add the capability to what you already run, measure the actual time recovered over a month, and only consider a full platform switch if the underlying system itself is the limiting factor.

The measurement worth doing before and after adoption is simple: compare total billed hours per fee earner for a representative month before the tool, against the same period after, adjusted for caseload. That comparison is a more honest test of value than any vendor's headline recovery percentage.
