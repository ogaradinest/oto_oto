What Clay actually does

You load a list, it finds and fills in the rest

Clay looks like a spreadsheet, which is the point, you're already comfortable in one. Drop in a list of companies or people, and it runs each row through 150-plus data providers (ZoomInfo, Hunter.io, People Data Labs, and more) to find emails, phone numbers, and company details. If one provider comes up empty, it tries the next automatically, a trick called "waterfalling" that's the main reason people pick Clay over a single lookup tool.

Picture a founder launching booking software for independent gyms. Instead of googling three hundred gym names one by one, they drop the list into Clay, which pulls each owner's verified email and whether they already use a competitor's software, then pushes the clean list into their cold email tool. A week of manual research becomes an afternoon.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

What does Clay actually do, in plain terms?

You give it a list of companies or people, and it automatically finds missing details like emails, phone numbers, and job titles by checking over 150 data sources, then hands you a clean list ready for outreach.

Is Clay worth it for a one-person startup?

The free plan's 100 credits are enough to test it on a small list. If you're sending more than a handful of cold emails a week, the time saved on manual research usually justifies the lowest paid tier.

Does Clay write the outreach emails too?

It can generate AI-written first lines and snippets, but a personalised human-reviewed message still outperforms generic AI copy, so treat Clay as the research engine, not the copywriter.

How is Clay different from just using LinkedIn and Google?

Clay automates the lookup across dozens of data providers at once and lets a single mistake, like a dead email, fall back to another source automatically, instead of you manually checking each one.

Why this matters for you

A small business without a sales team can't afford a researcher whose only job is finding emails, but it can't skip research either, since a list of guesses gets ignored or bounces. Clay turns that research into a background task, freeing the one or two people running the business to spend their time on the conversations the list makes possible.
