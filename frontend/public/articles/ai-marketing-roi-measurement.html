Why proving marketing ROI has actually gotten harder

More AI spend, less confidence in what it delivers

Only 41% of marketers can currently demonstrate ROI on their AI marketing investments, a figure that has fallen from 49% the year before even as AI marketing spend has grown. 40% of marketers more broadly say they find it difficult to prove the ROI of their marketing activities at all, and 62% of CMOs name proving marketing ROI to finance as their single biggest challenge, according to NIQ's 2026 CMO Outlook.

The root cause, according to MarTech.org's 2025 survey, is not a shortage of measurement models or sophistication. 65.7% of respondents named data integration, getting the numbers from different platforms to talk to each other cleanly, as their top martech management challenge, ahead of budget, skills, or tooling gaps.

Why last-touch attribution keeps winning despite being wrong

Familiar and simple beats accurate, until it does not

Last-touch attribution, crediting the final click before a sale to the entire purchase, still dominates at 67% adoption despite being widely acknowledged as ineffective, largely because it is the simplest model to set up and explain. Multi-touch attribution, which spreads credit across every interaction a customer had before buying, has grown from 31% adoption in 2023 to 47% in 2026, and companies making that switch report 15 to 30% lower customer acquisition costs and up to 40% ROI improvement.

Some businesses making the switch discover that as much as 60% of their previous spend was misallocated, credited to the wrong channel or campaign rather than genuinely wasted, which meant budget decisions were being made on a fundamentally inaccurate picture of what was actually working.

The tool that changes the maths for a small marketing team

Marketing mix modelling used to be a six-figure engagement

Marketing mix modelling, a statistical approach that measures the revenue impact of spend across channels including offline ones like TV, used to require a specialist consulting engagement costing well into six figures, putting it firmly out of reach for a small business. Google made its Meridian marketing mix modelling framework freely available to all marketers in January 2025, turning this into a project a small in-house team can run directly rather than an enterprise-only capability.

The two approaches, multi-touch attribution and marketing mix modelling, answer different questions and are increasingly used together rather than as alternatives: multi-touch attribution tells you whether a specific digital campaign is pulling its weight day to day, while marketing mix modelling tells you whether a broader shift in spend actually moved overall revenue.

A realistic starting point for a small marketing team

Fix data integration before adding a new model

Given that data integration, not model sophistication, is the top-cited barrier, the highest-value first step is making sure your existing marketing platforms, ad accounts, CRM and sales data are actually feeding into one place cleanly, before introducing multi-touch attribution or marketing mix modelling on top of an unreliable data foundation.

Once that foundation is in place, start with multi-touch attribution for a clearer read on your digital campaigns, since it requires less specialist setup than marketing mix modelling, and consider Google's free Meridian framework specifically once you have enough historical spend and revenue data, at least several months across multiple channels, for the model to produce a meaningful result.
