Why the first four weeks deserve the first investment

Fall-throughs cluster early

Fall-through data shows 38% of collapsed sales fail within four weeks of the sale being agreed, with weeks one and two alone accounting for nearly 16% of all collapses. Survey issues are the single leading cause of collapse at 37.5%, but delay itself is what turns a fixable problem into a lost sale: a buyer or seller who waits weeks for a routine check often has time to reconsider, get gazumped, or lose mortgage approval before anything is actually wrong with the property.

This is why the highest-leverage place for a small firm to apply AI is not the conveyancing process as a whole. It is the specific early steps, identity verification, source of funds, initial title checks, that determine how quickly a transaction gets past its riskiest window.

Why AML automation is the sensible first step

Cheapest to adopt, fastest to show a return

Specialist AML platforms built for conveyancing can cut the time spent on client due diligence by up to 80%, and one recent case study recorded a 350% improvement in verification sign-off efficiency after adoption. This is the easiest AI investment for a small firm to justify: the workflow is narrow and well-defined, the compliance requirement already exists regardless of AI, and the tool pays for itself in hours saved within the first few transactions.

Contract review and chain-management tools are valuable too, but they solve different problems later in the transaction. Starting with AML gets a firm comfortable with AI-assisted verification on a contained, low-risk process before extending automation to judgement-heavy areas like lease analysis.

The staffing reality behind this decision

Fewer people are available to absorb delay

The number of registered conveyancers in England and Wales fell to 3,425 by January 2026, down from 3,539 a year earlier, while solicitors working in residential conveyancing dropped to 10,724, over 2,000 fewer than at the end of 2021. A shrinking workforce handling a recovering transaction volume means fewer people available to manually chase identity documents and source-of-funds evidence.

Automating the most repetitive, lowest-judgement part of that workload, verification, frees the people you do have for the parts of a transaction that genuinely need a qualified conveyancer's attention: reviewing search results, advising on lease terms, managing a difficult chain.

What the SRA and CLC actually require

Principle-based, not tool-specific

Both the SRA and the CLC take a principle-based approach to AI and technology: firms must maintain client confidentiality, keep audit trails, ensure a person reviews any AI-assisted decision with real consequences, and can explain how the technology works. Neither regulator prescribes a specific tool or bans AI-assisted verification.

For a small firm, meeting this bar does not require a compliance department. It requires choosing an AML platform with a clear audit trail, keeping a named person accountable for reviewing flagged cases, and documenting that choice once, rather than treating every new client onboarding as a fresh judgement call.
