The same induction speech, ten times over

Hiring in a hurry usually means training badly

Take a small pack-and-dispatch warehouse near Milton Keynes. Nine permanent staff, no HR department, and every November the owner takes on fourteen seasonal pickers to get through the Christmas rush. For years that meant two full shifts per batch of new starters, the owner walking each one through the scanner app, the safety rules, the break times, and the same twenty questions, again and again.

The real cost wasn't visible on any single day. It showed up as picking mistakes in week one, running at around 9%, and as the odd new starter who accepted the job, then simply never turned up, leaving a gap on the rota with no warning.

What changed once the welcome ran itself

An AI onboarding assistant, not a bigger induction team

The owner built a simple AI onboarding assistant: a chatbot paired with a handful of short videos, sent to every new starter the moment they accepted the job, days before their first shift. It answered the obvious questions, walked through the scanner app step by step, and laid out exactly what to expect on day one, so arriving for a shift stopped feeling like a leap into the unknown.

Six weeks into this year's seasonal hiring, ramp-up time had dropped from two full shifts of shadowing to about half a shift. Week-one picking errors fell from roughly 9% to 3%. And the pre-day-one no-shows nearly disappeared, because candidates had already had a proper welcome, and a named contact to message, before they ever walked through the door.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Do I need an HR team or a dedicated trainer to set this up?

No. Most AI onboarding tools are built for businesses without one, and the warehouse above runs it with nine permanent staff and no HR function at all.

Is this worth it for just a handful of seasonal hires?

Often more so. The smaller the batch, the more your own time is worth protecting, and repeating the same induction ten times a season adds up fast.

Does an AI assistant replace someone welcoming new starters in person?

No. It handles the repeatable stuff, like FAQs, paperwork and the "where's the kettle" questions, so whoever greets them on day one can spend that time on what actually needs a human.

What's the fastest way to try this before a busy season hits?

Write down the ten questions every new starter asks you in their first shift, build a short FAQ or chatbot flow around just those, and see if ramp-up time drops before building out the rest.

Why this matters for you

A small business doesn't have a training department to absorb bad onboarding, it has the owner's own morning. Every hour spent repeating the same induction is an hour not spent on customers, orders or anything that actually grows the business, and with replacing a staff member running over £30,000 once everything is added up, a new starter who quietly leaves in week two is not a small loss to shrug off.
