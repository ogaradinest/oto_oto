Why AI due diligence tools are finally built for this deal size

Not every tool needs private equity pricing

Most of the earliest AI due diligence platforms targeted private equity firms and their Big Four advisers, running review processes priced for deals well above $100 million and firms doing dozens of transactions annually. Given that sub-£100 million deals make up 88% of UK M&A activity, that pricing model excluded the vast majority of actual transactions taking place.

A newer generation of tools, Robin AI and Diligen among them, is now explicitly positioned for sponsors running two to six deals a year and for transactions under $50 million, closing the gap between what most deals actually need and what was previously available only at enterprise pricing.

What AI actually changes in a due diligence process

Depth of coverage, not just speed

The most mature AI applications in due diligence are legal contract review, financial anomaly detection and customer cohort analysis. A language model trained on legal documents can extract key terms, flag non-standard clauses, identify change-of-control provisions, and summarise obligations across thousands of contracts in hours rather than days.

The genuine benefit is not primarily time saved, though AI-assisted review cuts due diligence time by an estimated 30 to 50%. It is that a deal team reviewing every document rather than a sample catches the problem contract or the anomalous customer concentration that a time-pressured manual review, sampling a fraction of the data room, might have missed entirely.

What this actually saves on a smaller deal

The adviser fee reduction matters more at this scale

A well-organised, AI-assisted due diligence data room can save $10,000 to $70,000 on a typical mid-market deal by cutting redundant adviser work, reviewing the same documents multiple times across different workstreams because information was not structured for reuse. For a smaller deal, this saving is proportionally far more significant against total transaction costs than it would be on a nine-figure acquisition.

The current UK M&A market reinforces why this matters now: deal volumes fell 30% year on year in the first quarter of 2026 while aggregate deal value rose 36%, meaning the deals that do progress are being scrutinised more carefully by buyers deploying capital selectively. Thorough, efficient due diligence is increasingly what separates a deal that completes from one that stalls.

A realistic way to use this on a smaller acquisition

Layer AI review under your existing adviser, do not replace them

For a business buying or selling in the sub-£10 million to £50 million range, the sensible approach is to use an AI-assisted platform to do the first-pass, full-coverage review, every contract, every anomaly, every cohort, and have your solicitor or accountant focus their time on the specific items the AI flags rather than starting from a blank page across the entire data room.

This does not remove the need for professional advisers. It changes what they spend their billable time on, from reading every document once to interrogating the specific handful of issues that actually matter to the deal.

The questions small business owners ask most

Is AI due diligence only useful for large private equity deals?

No. Newer tools are specifically priced and built for sponsors doing two to six deals a year and for transactions under $50 million, closing a gap that previously left most UK M&A activity, which is overwhelmingly sub-£100 million, without an affordable option.

Does AI due diligence replace lawyers and accountants on a deal?

No. It changes what they spend time on. AI handles the first-pass, full-coverage review across contracts and financials, and advisers focus their billable time on the specific issues flagged rather than reviewing every document from scratch.

How much time does AI actually save in due diligence?

Estimates put the reduction at 30 to 50% of review time, though the more valuable benefit is depth of coverage, examining every document rather than a sample, not simply finishing faster.

Why does due diligence quality matter more in the current UK market?

UK deal volumes fell 30% year on year in early 2026 while aggregate value rose 36%, meaning buyers are being more selective. Thorough due diligence increasingly determines which deals actually complete.
