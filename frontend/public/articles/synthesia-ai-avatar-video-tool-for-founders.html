What it's good for, and where it isn't

An avatar isn't a replacement for you everywhere

This is the right tool for repeatable, script-heavy content: product explainers, onboarding walkthroughs, training modules, FAQ videos, anything you'll need to update every few months. It's the wrong tool for a founder update on social media or an investor pitch, where people specifically want to see you, unscripted, reacting in real time. An avatar reads beautifully, but it doesn't riff.

Cost is where it earns its place for a small business. A free tier exists for testing short clips, and even a paid plan comes in well under the £2,960 a single agency-made explainer costs, before you even add a second language version or a mid-course update.

The questions small business owners ask most

Do I need to be on camera at all to use Synthesia?

No, that's the point. You can use a stock AI avatar, or build a digital version of your own likeness with consent if you specifically want your own face, but you never have to film yourself.

Will an AI avatar make my business look less trustworthy?

It depends what you use it for. For a training video or product walkthrough, most people don't mind at all. For anything meant to feel personal, like a founder update, a real unscripted video still lands better.

Can it handle languages other than English?

Yes, and it's one of its strongest features. Synthesia supports over 150 languages and accents, so one script can produce native-sounding versions for different markets without hiring a translator.

What does it cost for a solo founder?

There's a free tier for testing short clips, and paid plans start at a level most solo founders can absorb monthly, well under the cost of a single professionally filmed video.
