Start With an Honest Data Audit

You can't write the policy until you know what you're actually collecting

Before opening any AI tool, list every place personal data touches your business: the email signup form, Google Analytics, your payment processor, any chatbot or CRM you're testing. Most first-time founders underestimate this — a basic Shopify or Squarespace store easily touches five or six different tools before a single sale happens.

Write it in plain language: what you collect, why, where it's stored, and whether a third party (Stripe, Mailchimp, Google) ever sees it. That list is the real input for the AI step — a vague prompt produces a vague, non-compliant policy, no matter how good the tool is.

Draft It With AI, Then Check It Against GDPR

Generators and general AI tools both work, if you check the output

Feed your data list into a dedicated generator (Termly and similar tools offer free or low-cost UK-GDPR templates) or into ChatGPT or Claude with a structured prompt naming every item from your audit. Either route produces a workable first draft in minutes rather than the days a solicitor's queue usually takes.

Then check the draft against the ICO's own guidance: does it name a lawful basis for each type of data, explain how long you keep it, and give a clear way to contact you about it? AI tools are good at structure and plain English, but they don't know your actual retention period or whether you've registered with the ICO — you still fill in those specifics.

The Clause Most Founders Forget: Disclosing Your Own AI Use

A mini example: a one-person online store

Priya was three weeks from launching a handmade jewellery store. She used a generator to draft her policy in about 40 minutes, working from a list of what her site actually collected: Shopify checkout data, Klaviyo email data, and a Tidio chatbot reading customer messages. The generator's default template didn't mention the chatbot's AI provider — so she added one paragraph naming it, which is what the ICO expects for anything using automated processing of personal data.

That's the gap AI-generated policies most often leave: built from what's common, not what's actually running on your site. Cross-checking your own tool list against the draft, line by line, is the ten minutes that makes it accurate instead of just plausible.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Do I need a privacy policy before I have any customers?

Yes — if your site collects any personal data at all, including through an email signup form or analytics cookies, UK GDPR requires a policy from the moment the site goes live, not from your first sale.

Is an AI-generated privacy policy actually legally compliant?

It can be a solid, compliant baseline if you feed it an accurate list of what you collect and check it against ICO guidance — but it isn't a substitute for legal advice if your business handles anything unusual, like health data or under-18s.

What's the real difference between a privacy policy and terms & conditions?

A privacy policy explains what personal data you collect and why; terms & conditions cover the rules of using your product or site, like payment, returns, and liability. Most new businesses need both, and they're separate documents.

Do I have to mention AI tools, like a chatbot, in my policy?

Yes, if that tool processes customer data — the ICO expects this disclosed, and is publishing further guidance on automated decision-making in 2026, so keep this section easy to update.

For a founder bootstrapping a launch, £150-£280 an hour for a solicitor isn't a rounding error — it's real runway. An AI-assisted draft, built from an honest audit of your own tools, gets you a compliant document for the cost of an afternoon instead of a solicitor's invoice.
