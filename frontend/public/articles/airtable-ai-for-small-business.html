What Airtable AI Actually Does

Part spreadsheet, part database, part assistant that fills in the boring bits

Open a base in Airtable and it looks like a spreadsheet at first, rows and columns. The difference shows once you link tables: "Orders" can pull straight from "Customers" and "Stock", so updating one updates all three. The AI layer sits on top, drafting a field's content or answering a plain-English question about your own data, no formula required.

Picture a three-person events-hire startup running bookings in one spreadsheet, inventory in another, contracts in a shared drive nobody can search. They rebuilt both as one linked Airtable base in a weekend, and an AI field started flagging any booking needing more marquees than they had in stock, catching a double-booking two weeks before the event.

What It Costs, and the Bit Nobody Mentions

Free to start, but the real cost is the hour you skip setting it up properly

Airtable's free plan covers individuals and very small teams, no card required. Past that, the Team plan runs $20 per user a month billed annually, charged only for people with editing rights on a base.

The catch: Airtable is flexible enough to let you build badly. Sketch which tables you actually need, Customers, Orders, Stock, on paper first, or you'll be rebuilding your base from scratch by month two.
