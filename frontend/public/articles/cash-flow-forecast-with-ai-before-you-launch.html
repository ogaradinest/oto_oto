The forecast in your pitch deck isn't the one that saves you

Investors want the story, your bank account wants the truth

Pitch deck financials sell a vision: revenue climbing, costs flat, everyone paying on time. A cash flow forecast does something less glamorous and far more useful, it tells you which week you'll be short. Both can coexist, but only one needs updating every Friday.

Take a plausible scenario: a founder opens a small bakery supplying three local cafés on 30-day terms. Sales look healthy from day one, around £4,200 a month. But flour, wages and rent are paid weekly or monthly upfront, while the cafés don't settle up for 30 to 45 days. Without mapping that gap, the founder runs out of working capital in week seven, not because the business isn't working, but because nobody modelled the lag between doing the work and getting paid for it.

Building the forecast with AI, step by step

You don't need Excel wizardry, you need the right questions answered in order

Start by listing every cost you already know: rent, software, wages, stock, insurance, loan repayments. Feed that list into an AI tool such as ChatGPT or Claude and ask it to build a simple 13-week rolling cash flow template, with columns for expected income, expected outgoings and running balance. Be specific about payment terms, that's the detail most generic templates miss.

Once you're trading and have accounting software connected, tools built for this take over the heavy lifting. Xero's forecasting features and add-ons like Fathom now generate AI commentary straight from your live numbers, flagging a coming shortfall weeks before it happens rather than after the direct debit bounces.

Quick answers

The questions most founders ask first

Do I need accounting software before I can forecast cash flow?

No. A spreadsheet and a free AI tool like ChatGPT or Claude can build your first 13-week forecast before you've even registered the business.

How far ahead should a new business forecast?

A rolling 13-week view for day-to-day decisions, plus a 12-month version broken down by month for your first year.

Can AI actually predict my cash flow accurately?

Not on its own. It only works with the numbers you give it, so treat it as a fast way to build and update the model, not a crystal ball.

What's the biggest mistake founders make with cash flow forecasts?

Forecasting sales but forgetting payment timing. A sale isn't cash until it lands in the account.

Why this matters for you

For a small business, the cash buffer is thin by definition. A single late-paying client or an unexpectedly large tax bill can tip a genuinely profitable business into crisis. A forecast doesn't fix that fragility, but it turns a surprise into something you saw coming three weeks ago and already had a plan for.
