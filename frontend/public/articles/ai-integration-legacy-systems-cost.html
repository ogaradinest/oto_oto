Why the AI tool is rarely the expensive part

Integration, not the model, is where budgets actually break

68% of AI projects exceed their initial budget estimate, with the average overrun reaching 42% above the original figure, and some projects run 30 to 70% over depending on the specific systems involved. 85% of organisations misestimate AI project costs by more than 10%, and a meaningful share miss their forecast by over half. Integration complexity, connecting the new AI tool to systems that were never built to talk to it, is consistently cited as one of the top three cost escalators in enterprise surveys.

The pattern behind this is straightforward: a small, simple AI agent can cost around $25,000 to build in isolation, but total first-year costs for AI agent integration typically range from $12,000 to $180,000 depending on how many existing systems it needs to connect to and how complex those connections turn out to be.

What actually drives the integration cost specifically

Old systems without APIs are the biggest single factor

Legacy system integration, building custom middleware for older platforms that were never designed with an API for another system to connect to, typically adds $30,000 to $100,000 on top of the core AI tool cost. The underlying difficulties are consistent: data scattered across several systems with inconsistent formats, poor documentation requiring reverse engineering of how an old system actually works, and a shrinking pool of engineers who understand platforms built years or decades ago.

Middleware and integration platform-as-a-service tools can reduce the amount of custom development needed, but they carry their own upfront cost, typically $20,000 to $100,000, and the more separate systems involved, accounting software, a CRM, a booking platform, a stock system, the higher the total integration cost climbs, because each connection is its own separate piece of work.

The cost that continues after launch

Integration is not a one-off expense

Software maintenance for an AI-integrated system typically runs 15 to 25% of the initial build cost every year, meaning a $25,000 integration carries an ongoing $3,750 to $6,250 annual cost simply to keep it working as the connected systems themselves get updated. This ongoing cost is frequently missing from a first-year budget built only around the initial build.

This matters specifically because the systems on the other end of an integration, your accounting software, your CRM, your booking platform, are updated independently by their own vendors, and each of those updates carries a real chance of breaking the connection your AI tool depends on, a cost that a one-off project budget does not naturally account for.

A more realistic way to budget for integration

Ask about the connection cost before the tool cost

Before committing to any AI tool that needs to connect to your existing systems, get a specific, written estimate for the integration work separately from the cost of the AI tool itself, and ask directly whether any of your systems lack a modern API, since that single factor is the clearest predictor of a $30,000 to $100,000 cost addition.

Budget for ongoing maintenance from day one, 15 to 25% of the build cost annually, rather than treating it as a surprise bill in year two, and build in a contingency reflecting the 42% average overrun reported industry-wide rather than assuming your project will be the exception.

The questions small business owners ask most

Why do AI projects go over budget so often?

68% of AI projects exceed their initial estimate, averaging 42% over budget, and integration with existing systems, not the AI tool itself, is one of the top three cost escalators cited in industry surveys.

How much does it cost to integrate AI with an old system that has no API?

Typically $30,000 to $100,000 in additional custom middleware work, on top of the core AI tool cost, because of scattered data, poor documentation and the specialist engineering time needed to work with older platforms.

Does AI integration cost end once the system is built?

No. Ongoing maintenance typically runs 15 to 25% of the initial build cost every year, since the systems your AI tool connects to are updated independently and can break that connection.

What is the single most useful question to ask before an AI integration project?

Whether any of the systems involved lack a modern API. That single factor is the clearest predictor of a significant additional cost, and should be priced separately from the AI tool itself.

Why this matters for you

A small business is more likely to run older accounting, booking or stock systems without modern APIs than a business built on newer, cloud-native software, making the $30,000 to $100,000 legacy integration cost a real risk rather than an edge case. Asking for a separate, specific integration estimate before committing to any AI tool, and budgeting for the 15 to 25% annual maintenance cost from the start, avoids the most common way small business AI budgets run over.
