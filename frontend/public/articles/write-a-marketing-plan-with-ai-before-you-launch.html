Why the Marketing Page Gets Skipped

It's not laziness, it's that nobody taught you the format

Most founders can describe their product for ten minutes straight. Ask who the first fifty customers are and how they'll hear about the business, and the answers go vague fast. A marketing plan is five decisions written down: who you're selling to, what you're saying, which channel you'll use, what you'll spend, and how you'll know it's working. Most founders never get past the first one, and that's the gap AI is genuinely good at closing, not by inventing the strategy, but by forcing the five answers in order instead of three vague sentences about social media.

Draft It With AI, Five Questions at a Time

One prompt per section, in the order a real plan needs them

Work through it as five short prompts rather than one giant request: describe your product and best guess at who buys it first; ask where that customer already spends attention, not a generic "social media and SEO" answer; ask for one sentence on what makes you different, in the customer's words; turn the channels into a monthly plan with what it costs; then ask what single number you'll check weekly.

Push back on anything generic. "Post consistently on Instagram" with no what, how often, or why isn't a plan, it's a placeholder. Make it justify every channel against your actual customer.

A Dog-Walking App, Built in an Afternoon

A mini example of the five prompts in order

Picture a founder building a local dog-walking app, pre-launch, no budget. The five prompts gave her: first customers are owners in two postcodes who already pay for a walker; they're in local Facebook groups and vet waiting rooms, not generic ads; the message is "book your regular walker in under a minute"; the monthly plan is three Facebook posts and a flyer in two vet practices, under £30; and the number to watch is app downloads per week, not a vague feeling about "visibility". One afternoon, not a sprint billed by an agency.

Quick Answers

The questions founders ask most

Will an AI-written marketing plan actually be specific to my business?

Only if you feed it specifics back. A generic prompt gets a generic plan; real numbers get something usable.

Do I need marketing experience to do this?

No. The AI supplies the structure; you supply the facts about your product and customer. That's what makes it work.

How often should I update the plan once I've launched?

Monthly for the first few months. What you guessed before launch is rarely exactly right once real people start buying.

What if AI suggests a channel I can't afford?

Say so, and ask for a cheaper alternative aimed at the same customer. The point is a plan you'll actually run.

Why this matters for you

A funded startup can afford to get the marketing plan wrong for six months while an agency finds the right channel. A business running on savings and a launch date can't. A specific plan on paper before you spend a pound on ads turns marketing from a monthly guess into something you can test, and the planning cost is now an afternoon with AI instead of a retainer you can't justify yet.
