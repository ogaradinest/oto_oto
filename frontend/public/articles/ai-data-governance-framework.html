AI governance conversations tend to focus on the model: is it biased, is it explainable, is it compliant. Less attention goes to the data feeding that model, even though data quality is where most AI failures actually start. That's starting to change, and the numbers behind the shift are worth understanding before your business becomes a data point in next year's version of this research.

Information integrity risk, AI-enabled decisions made on untrustworthy or opaque data, is now the number-one concern among risk professionals, ranking above cybersecurity, regulatory risk, and geopolitical uncertainty, according to Gartner's Q1 2026 research.

Why Data Governance Overtook Cybersecurity as a Worry

The risk moved upstream, from the model to what feeds it

Gartner's research tracking risk professional sentiment through early 2026 found information integrity risk, the risk of AI systems making decisions on data that's untrustworthy or opaque, has become the single top concern, overtaking cybersecurity, regulatory risk, and geopolitical uncertainty. That ranking reflects a real shift: as AI systems make more decisions with less direct human review, the quality of the data behind those decisions matters more than almost anything else.

The Governance Platform Gap

A measurable difference between structured and unstructured approaches

A Gartner survey of 360 organisations found that those using a dedicated AI governance platform are 3.4 times more likely to achieve high effectiveness in AI governance than those relying on informal or ad hoc processes. That's not a marginal improvement. It's the difference between governance that actually catches problems and governance that exists mainly on paper.

The market has responded accordingly: AI governance spending is projected to reach $492 million in 2026 and surpass $1 billion by 2030, as more organisations move from informal policy documents to structured platforms and processes.

The Regulatory Backdrop Isn't Slowing Down

Fragmented rules, real penalties

Gartner predicts that by 2030, fragmented AI regulation will extend to 75% of the world's economies, driving roughly $1 billion in total compliance spend globally. In the EU specifically, penalties under the AI Act can reach €35 million or 7% of global annual revenue, whichever is higher, for the most serious breaches. Whatever your view on the pace of regulation, the direction is consistent: more jurisdictions, more specific requirements, and higher stakes for getting data handling wrong.

A Framework That Doesn't Require an Enterprise Budget

Four things worth doing regardless of company size

You don't need a $492 million market's worth of tooling to apply the same principles at a smaller scale. Know what data feeds each AI system you use. Know where that data came from and whether it's current. Know who's responsible for checking it. And review that list on a schedule, not only when something goes wrong. That's the same structure the enterprise platforms formalise, just without the price tag.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

What does 'information integrity risk' actually mean in practice?

It means an AI system is making a decision, a recommendation, a categorisation, a response, based on data that's outdated, incomplete, or simply wrong, without anyone catching it before the decision reaches a customer or a report.

Do we need a dedicated governance platform as a small business?

Not necessarily a paid platform, but the discipline it represents, knowing your data sources, their owners, and their freshness, applies at any size, even if you track it in a spreadsheet rather than dedicated software.

Does the EU AI Act apply if we're a UK-only business?

Only if you sell into the EU or your AI tools are provided by an EU-based vendor subject to the Act. UK-only businesses are more directly affected by the ICO's own code of practice and existing UK data protection law.

What's the fastest way to check our own data governance gap?

List every AI tool you use, and for each one, write down where its data comes from and who last checked that it's accurate. Gaps in that list are your actual governance gap, not a hypothetical one.

Why this matters for you

The $1 billion figures in this research describe enterprise spending, but the underlying risk, AI decisions built on bad data, applies just as much to a small business using a single AI-powered tool. If a lead-scoring tool works from an outdated customer list, or a scheduling assistant works from a calendar nobody's cleaned up in months, the AI isn't wrong, your data was. That's a fixable problem, and fixing it costs nothing but attention.

Try this next

Pick the one AI tool your business relies on most, and check where its data actually comes from. If that data hasn't been reviewed or cleaned in the last three months, do that first, before evaluating whether the tool itself is working properly. Most 'AI isn't accurate enough' complaints trace back to this exact gap.
