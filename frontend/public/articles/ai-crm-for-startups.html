Why this trips up founders especially

Spreadsheets work fine, until they suddenly don't

In the excitement of launching, leads land everywhere at once: a DM, a contact-form email, a number scribbled after a networking event. At five contacts that's manageable from memory. At thirty, it isn't — and nobody notices the system has failed until a good lead has already gone cold.

Take a freelance graphic designer setting up her own studio. In her first two months she picks up 40 enquiries from Instagram DMs, her website's contact form, and word of mouth. By month three she can't remember who she quoted, who ghosted, and who's still waiting on a reply — and loses three warm leads worth roughly £4,200 in potential work, simply because nobody followed up in time.

Setting one up takes an afternoon, not a sprint

Five steps, and none of them need code

Pick a free AI-native CRM — HubSpot's free tier, Notion with a CRM template, or Attio all suit a pre-revenue business. Import your existing contacts from email or your phone. Set three or four pipeline stages: enquired, quoted, followed up, won or lost. Turn on the AI features — most free tiers now draft follow-ups, summarise calls, and flag contacts who've gone quiet. Then set one rule for yourself: every enquiry gets a reply within the hour, even if it's just "got this, more details by Friday."

What the AI part is actually doing

It's not just storage — it nudges you before you drop the ball

The database part of a CRM is the boring bit computers have handled for decades. What's changed is the AI layer on top: it drafts a follow-up in your voice, scores which leads are likeliest to convert, and flags anyone who's gone quiet for a week. None of that replaces judgement, but it replaces the mental load of remembering everyone yourself — the part that actually breaks past a handful of contacts.

Quick Answers

The questions founders ask most

Do I need to pay for a CRM before I have any revenue?

No. Most AI-native CRM tools — HubSpot's free tier, Notion, Attio's free plan — cover everything a pre-revenue founder needs. Upgrade once you outgrow the free contact limit.

Isn't a spreadsheet good enough when I'm just starting out?

It's fine for a handful of contacts, but it won't remind you to follow up or flag who's gone quiet — that gap is what an AI-native CRM closes.

Which CRM should I actually pick?

For most first-time founders, HubSpot's free CRM or a Notion CRM template are the easiest starting points — both have AI built in and cost nothing at your stage.

How long does this really take to set up?

Realistically, an afternoon: import your contacts, set three or four pipeline stages, and turn on AI follow-up prompts.

Why this matters for you

For a business that doesn't officially exist yet, the enquiries you're gathering now are your entire pipeline — there's no existing client base to cushion a dropped lead. Getting organised before day one, rather than after a missed follow-up costs you your first client, is cheap insurance for a new founder.
