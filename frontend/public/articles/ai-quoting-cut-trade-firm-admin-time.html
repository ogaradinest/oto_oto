The kitchen-table quote backlog

Why the slowest part of the job isn't the job

Take a small heating and plumbing outfit, three people, call it Dave's firm. Dave and his team see four or five jobs a day. Each one needs a quote: parts, labour, a buffer for whatever's behind the wall. Written properly, that's 20 to 30 minutes per quote, done from memory after hours because there's no time during the day.

The maths catches up fast: five site visits means two hours of evening admin most nights, on top of the actual plumbing. Some quotes slip a day. A few slip further. And the customer who gets a quote back within the hour, even from a pricier competitor, often books before Dave's version lands in their inbox.

What changed when the quote took five minutes

Same information, a different starting point

The fix wasn't a new pricing model, it was a different first draft. Instead of writing from a blank page, Dave now dictates a voice note on site — parts, hours, complications — and an AI quoting tool turns it into a formatted quote with his standard rates already applied. He still checks every line and adjusts price before it goes out. What took 25 minutes now takes closer to 5.

Over a month, that's roughly 6 hours a week back, mostly evening time. The bigger shift was speed to the customer: quotes that used to go out the next day now go out within the hour, while the job is still fresh in the customer's mind. Win rate isn't something you can pin on one change, but faster quotes consistently mean more of them get accepted, simply because Dave's firm is first in the inbox.

Quick Answers

The questions small business owners ask most

Doesn't AI-written quoting risk sending out wrong prices?

Only if nobody checks it. Treat the output as a first draft: it pulls in your rates and structure, you still confirm every price before it's sent.

What kind of tool does this?

Voice-to-quote apps built for trades, or a general AI assistant set up with your rate card and template. Trades-specific tools need less setup.

Is this only useful for trades businesses?

No. Any small business that writes repeat documents — quotes, estimates, proposals — benefits from the same pattern: dictate the details, let AI draft it, you check and send.

How much does something like this cost?

Most trades-focused quoting tools run £20 to £50 a month, well inside what one extra job a month would cover.

Why this matters for you

For a small business, the value isn't the time saved on any one quote, it's what stacks up over a month: fewer evenings lost to paperwork, fewer quotes that slip and never get sent, and a faster response than competitors still writing theirs from scratch. None of that needs a big budget or an IT team, just one document you already write often and a tool that drafts it faster than you can from memory.

Try this next

This week, pick the document you write most often — quote, estimate, or proposal — and time yourself writing the next one from scratch. Then dictate the same details into an AI tool and see how much of the draft it gets right. That gap is roughly what you'd get back every week.
